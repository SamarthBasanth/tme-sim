# Does a DISCRETE vessel network change the model's conclusions?

**The single highest-value structural fix in `results/SESSION_AUDIT.md` §3, built and then measured.** The drug source in this model is a 40 µm-bandwidth Gaussian KDE of measured endothelial-cell positions. This report replaces it with discrete segmented vessels — a source that is **exactly zero** away from a vessel — and quantifies what moves.

Generated by `tmesim.validation.vessel_network_impact` in 2815 s on the REAL 10x Xenium GBM section (`reconstruct_real('xenium', dx_um=12)`, grid 424×675, 40,887 measured cells, 30,174 tumour). 3 source fields × 3 drugs = 9 transport solves. Figure: `vessel_network_impact.png`. Modules: `reconstruct/vessel_network.py`, `validation/vessel_network_impact.py`.

## Verdict, up front

**Every structural claim below is scored on arm C, not arm B.** B and A do not deliver the same total drug (3.36×), so an A→B difference is part geometry and part dose. C holds the total fixed and changes only *where* the drug enters. Reporting A→B as the structural effect would have overstated it **35×** on the headline metric (§6) — this is the single most important methodological point in the report.

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| Does the **flat floor** disappear? | **NO** — far-field (>150 µm) exposure is 25.99% of perivascular under A and 23.18% under B, a change of only 1.12× |
| Do **drugs separate** on fitted decay length? | **YES**, and by more than the audit hoped — cross-drug spread 3.12× under A → 6.78× under C, concentrated in doxorubicin (L 96 → 41 µm) |
| How much does **therapeutic coverage @1× IC50** move? | at most **+0.0166** absolute (A→C, geometry alone) — inside the ±0.03 grid-convergence band. The naive A→B number is -0.5855 — 35× larger, and almost entirely a dose artefact |
| Is **delivery still never the binding constraint**? | **broadly yes, it still is not** — worst near-minus-far coverage gap 0.110 (A) → 0.170 (C), a 1.55× widening |
| Should the discrete field become the **default**? | **NO — not yet**, see §8 |

**Two things must be said before any of this is read.**

**(1) The premise of the audit does not reproduce.** `SESSION_AUDIT.md` §3 and `delivery_metrics.md` report that *every* drug fits L = 26.5–28.2 µm against a source null of 23.7 µm. On the current reconstruction that is **not true**: on the same legacy distance axis this run gets temozolomide 178, cisplatin 109, doxorubicin 58 µm — a 3.1× spread — against a source null of 21.3 µm. §3 traces this to **audit fix #7**, the tissue-mask change, and demonstrates it: refitting the source null under the retired occupancy mask gives **23.68 µm**, i.e. the published 23.7 µm to three significant figures. *The 'all drugs give the same L' defect was cured by a fix that had nothing to do with vasculature, and the audit's §3 is stale on that point.* No vessel network was needed.

**(2) The fix is real but narrow.** The discrete field genuinely changes distance-resolved quantities — it roughly doubles the cross-drug spread in fitted decay length and, uniquely, leaves **no source-null length scale at all** for a drug L to be confounded with (§5). It does *not* change the model's headline conclusions: coverage moves by ≤0.0166 at matched dose, the far-field floor survives (§4), and no tumour cell population goes drug-free (§7). Recommendation and reasoning in §8.

## 1. Segmentation — discrete vessels from the measured endothelium

`segment_vessels` runs single-linkage clustering (equivalent to `DBSCAN(eps, min_samples=1)`, so no cell is discarded as noise) over the measured positions of the **3,984 `CellType.ENDOTHELIAL` cells** at a linking distance of **20 µm**.

**Why 20 µm and not a guess.** Volume electron microscopy of human cortex gives ~**20 endothelial cells per mm of vasculature** and **1–2 EC per capillary cross-section** (Shapson-Coe A et al., *Science* 2024;384(6696):eadk4858, [PMID 38723085](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38723085/); Sargent S et al., *Vascular Biology* 2023;5:e230010, [PMID 37582180](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37582180/)) — i.e. **one EC ≈ 50 µm of vessel length**, so consecutive nuclei on one vessel wall are tens of µm apart, and a 20 µm link joins cells that are closer than one EC's worth of vessel. Independently, `results/vascular_validation.md` §3.2 measures a median nearest-other-endothelial-cell distance of **26.4 µm** on this very section and §3.3 finds same-type enrichment of **2.8× within 10 µm and 2.8× at 10–20 µm**, decaying to the null by ~60 µm. 20 µm sits inside that enriched shell. The same paper's tissue-counting endothelial volume fraction, **8.0%**, is what our reconstruction's **9.7%** was already validated against.

Segmenting the endothelium into objects does not create the vascular hotspots and avascular zones that define GBM — **those were never in the cell positions to begin with**, so no source-field construction can recover them.

## 2. The two source fields, and what their numbers mean

| | A · KDE (current default) | B · discrete | C · discrete, dose-matched |
|---|---|---|---|
| construction | 40 µm Gaussian KDE of endothelial positions, ÷ its own max | 1.0 within 7.5 µm of a segmented vessel, **exactly 0.0** elsewhere | B × (A's total ÷ B's total) |
| % of tissue with a nonzero source | 99.71% | 2.50% | 2.50% |
| % of tissue with **exactly zero** source | 0.29% | 97.50% | 97.50% |
| mean over tissue | 0.0840 | 0.0250 | 0.0840 |
| max | 1.000 | 1.000 | 3.359 |
| tissue-integrated total (relative) | 16,269 | 4,843 | 16,269 |

**Normalisation, stated plainly.** Both fields are *relative*, neither is an absolute perfused fraction, and no number in this project should be read as an absolute delivery prediction. But they are relative to different things, and that difference is not cosmetic:

* **A**: `1.0` = *the densest endothelial-KDE spot anywhere in this particular section*. Change the section and the unit changes.
* **B**: `1.0` = *a perfused vessel wall leaking at the reference rate*, and `0.0` = *no vessel*. The engine's delivery term is `vascular_density × bbb_penetrance × plasma`, so B reads as "drug enters the interstitium at the plasma-driven leak rate at the vessel wall and nowhere else" — which is what a leaky-vessel boundary condition says. The unit is section-independent.
* **C**: B rescaled so the tissue-integrated total matches A. A delivers **3.36× more total drug into the tissue than B** at the same plasma concentration, purely because a max-normalised KDE spread over 100% of the tissue integrates to more than a unit source on 2.5% of it. **Any A→B difference is therefore part geometry and part dose. C removes the dose part.** Its cost: the in-vessel amplitude becomes 3.36, and because the vascular field is also the O₂ and IFP source, C is a slightly hyperoxic tissue. Both arms are reported; neither is hidden.

**Vessel radius = 7.5 µm** — the low end of measured human brain vessel caliber (15.0 ± 5.1 µm diameter in white matter to 37.4 ± 12.9 µm in cortical grey matter, *all brain tumours larger than normal-appearing tissue*: Hsu YY et al., *J Magn Reson Imaging* 2009;30(5):1078-84, [PMID 19856441](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19856441/); 18.1 ± 2.4 µm diameter in orthotopic U87 glioma validated against IHC: Kording F et al., *JMRI* 2014;40(6):1310-8, [PMID 24390982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24390982/)). **Honest caveat:** at dx = 12 µm one grid cell has an equivalent disc radius of 6.8 µm, so 7.5 µm is as much a statement about the mesh as about biology — it is the smallest radius that reliably paints ≥1 grid cell per vessel while staying inside the published range. The quantity that validates the choice is the area fraction in §1.2, and it passes to 1.19×.

## 3. First: arm A does NOT reproduce the defect it is supposed to have

A fix is only measurable against a reproduced baseline, so the first thing this study did was re-measure the status quo. It does not reproduce, and chasing that down changed what the whole exercise means.

On the **legacy distance axis** — the 95th-percentile threshold on the KDE that `vessel_profile` / `delivery_metrics` use, 5.0% of tissue area, identical construction — arm A gives:

| drug | L (µm), this run | L in `delivery_metrics.md` |
|---|---|---|
| temozolomide | **178.3** | 26.5–28.2 (five-drug panel) |
| cisplatin | **108.6** | 26.5–28.2 (five-drug panel) |
| doxorubicin | **58.0** | 26.5–28.2 (five-drug panel) |
| **source null** | **21.29** | **23.7** |
| *cross-drug spread* | **3.07×** | 1.07× |

The drugs span 3.1× here against 1.07× as published, and every value is 2–6× longer. **The defect the vessel network was built to fix is largely gone already.**

### 3.1 Why — and it is not this study's doing

The cause is **audit fix #7**, the tissue mask. `Domain.tissue_mask` changed from the grid-dependent `'occupancy'` rule (grid cells containing ≥1 measured cell) to the dx-stable `'geometric'` rule (union of 30 µm discs around measured cells). Every distance-from-vessel profile is binned over that mask, so changing it changes every fitted length. `delivery_metrics.md` was generated under the old mask.

This is testable **without any transport solve**, because the source null is a fit to `vascular_density` itself:

| tissue mask | tissue px | % of bounding box | max distance from a p95 vessel (µm) | **fitted source-null L (µm)** | source floor_fraction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `geometric` ← current default | 193,752 | 67.7% | 1203 | **21.29** | 0.150 |
| `occupancy` ← retired (fix #7) | 39,756 | 13.9% | 2347 | **23.68** | 0.146 |
| *published in `delivery_metrics.md`* | | | | *23.7* | *~0.12* |

**The occupancy mask returns 23.68 µm — the published 23.7 µm to three significant figures.** The discrepancy is fully explained by the mask. Nothing about the vessel network is involved.

**Consequence, stated plainly: `SESSION_AUDIT.md` §3 bullet 1 is stale.** "Fitted penetration length is 26–28 µm for ALL drugs" was a property of the retired tissue mask, not (only) of the KDE bandwidth. It was already fixed, by a change made for unrelated reasons, before this module existed. `results/delivery_metrics.md` should be re-run and its §4 and discrimination table corrected — the metric `penetration_length_L` is listed there as "does NOT discriminate" on a 1.07× spread, and on the current reconstruction its spread is 3.1×. **That is a claim in a published results file that is now wrong in the conservative direction, and finding it is arguably the most useful thing this study did.**

Everything from here on uses a **common distance axis**: Euclidean distance from a **segmented discrete vessel** (4,843 vessel pixels). It is the only axis on which "distance from a vessel" means the same thing in both arms — the legacy axis is a percentile threshold on A's own field, so it is not defined for B, and it measures distance from a *KDE hotspot*, not from a vessel (`vascular_validation.md` §5, which measures the two definitions to disagree by 3.0×).

## 4. Does the FLAT FLOOR disappear?

Two measures, one fitted and one fit-free, so the answer cannot be an artefact of the fit:

* `floor_fraction` — from `C(d) = C₀·exp(−d/L) + floor`, reported as `floor/(C₀+floor)`. This is the statistic `dose_schedule.md` quotes as ~0.12 (on the legacy KDE-hotspot axis). **On the vessel-based axis it collapses to ~0 for almost every drug/arm, and that is a fit failure, not a finding**: the profile decays gently over the whole available range, so the three-parameter form absorbs the plateau into a long `L` instead of into `floor`. Reported for continuity; do not read it.
* **empirical floor** — count-weighted mean of the profile over bins beyond 150 µm ÷ the perivascular (0–10 µm) bin. No functional form assumed, so nothing to mis-fit. **This is the number to read.**

| field | drug | fitted floor_fraction | **empirical floor** (>150 µm / perivascular) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (KDE) | temozolomide | 0.000 | **41.56%** |
|  | cisplatin | 0.000 | **26.95%** |
|  | doxorubicin | 0.000 | **9.45%** |
| A (KDE) — *the source itself* | (source null) | 0.000 | 0.0325% |
| B (discrete) | temozolomide | 0.000 | **40.12%** |
|  | cisplatin | 0.000 | **23.72%** |
|  | doxorubicin | 0.151 | **5.69%** |
| B (discrete) — *the source itself* | (source null) | — | 0.0000% |
| C (discrete, dose-matched) | temozolomide | 0.000 | **40.44%** |
|  | cisplatin | 0.000 | **23.94%** |
|  | doxorubicin | 0.147 | **5.75%** |
| C (discrete, dose-matched) — *the source itself* | (source null) | — | 0.0000% |

**The source-null rows are the finding.** The KDE source has a far-field floor of 0.03% of its perivascular value; the discrete source has 0.0000% — **exactly zero, by construction**. So the fix does exactly what it was designed to do *at the source*.

**But it does not carry through to the exposure.** The far-field exposure floor is 25.99% of perivascular under A and 23.18% under B — a change of only 1.12×. Removing every trace of source from 97.5% of the tissue barely moves the *exposure* there.

**Why, and it is not a bug — it is arithmetic.** Two length scales decide whether the *shape* of a source can survive into the exposure field, and both are computed below from the compiled drug parameters the solves actually used:

| drug | D_eff (µm²/s) | loss rate k (1/s) | **L_rd = √(D/k)** (µm) | **L_diff = √(4DT)** over 6 h (µm) | vs 40 µm intervessel gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| temozolomide | 265 | 0.00302 | **296** | **4,788** | 7× / 119× |
| cisplatin | 225 | 0.00879 | **160** | **4,413** | 4× / 110× |
| doxorubicin | 176 | 0.0333 | **73** | **3,903** | 2× / 97× |

`L_rd` is the steady-state reaction-diffusion penetration length — the only length that can hold a concentration difference open against tissue loss indefinitely. `L_diff` is how far drug spreads by diffusion over the exposure window. **Both are one to two orders of magnitude larger than the gap between segmented vessels.** The interstitium therefore equilibrates between vessels long before the window closes, and a source that is exactly zero produces an exposure that is nowhere near zero.

The flat floor in `dose_schedule.md` was attributed to the KDE bandwidth. This run shows the bandwidth was the *visible* cause but not the *sufficient* one: the floor is mostly **diffusion across a densely and near-uniformly vascularised section**, and it survives deleting the KDE entirely. That is a different — and considerably more robust — conclusion than the audit anticipated, and it means the finding 'delivery is never the binding constraint *on this section*' was not an artefact of our own smoothing after all.

## 5. Do the drugs finally SEPARATE?

The decisive test the audit specified: under the KDE every drug fitted 26–28 µm against a 23.7 µm source null, i.e. the number was measuring the source, not the drug. **§3 has already shown that premise no longer holds** — it was a property of the retired tissue mask — so what follows measures the residual effect of source geometry on top of that. Each arm is scored against **its own** source null.

**The discrete arm's source null is `undefined`, and that is the point.** The identical three-parameter fit cannot be applied to the discrete source because it is identically zero beyond one bin — there is no decay length in it to confound the drug's. Under A the null is a real, competing explanation; under B there is nothing left to explain the drug's L except transport.

**And the drugs do separate — but the honest reading is more complicated than "the fix worked".** Three things are true at once:

1. They **already separate under A** on a vessel-based axis: 3.12× across the three drugs. Combined with §3, the audit's "every drug gives 26–28 µm" is not a live defect on the current reconstruction under either source model.
2. The discrete field **roughly doubles** that separation, to 6.78× at matched dose. The effect is concentrated in the worst-penetrating drug: doxorubicin's L falls 96 → 41 µm (2.4×) while temozolomide's barely moves (299 → 276 µm). That is the physically expected pattern: a smooth source flatters exactly the drug that cannot travel, because it hands that drug a source everywhere. **Under the KDE the least-penetrating drug is the most flattered**, which is the same directional bias `vascular_validation.md` §5 predicted from the geometry alone.
3. It is **pure geometry, not dose**: L is scale-invariant, and arms B and C (which differ only by a 3.36× dose factor) agree on L to within 2.7%.

So the discrete field earns its place for *distance-resolved* claims — it both removes the null and widens the real signal — without earning the right to be the default (§6, §8).

Quartile median distances: 20, 35, 53, 82 µm.

**Nobody goes without.** The essentially-zero fraction is 0.11%–0.17% of tumour cells in **every arm**, and no cell in any arm receives exactly zero. Deleting the source from 97.5% of the tissue does not create a single drug-free tumour cell. That is the cleanest single statement of the §4 result.

**"Delivery is never the binding constraint" broadly SURVIVES the fix — with one real exception.** At matched dose the worst near-minus-far coverage gap goes from 0.110 (A) to 0.170 (C), a 1.55× widening, and the far quartile's mean exposure is still 0.34–0.74× the near quartile's. Temozolomide is unaffected (it never clears IC50 anywhere) and cisplatin is unaffected (it clears IC50 everywhere).

**The exception is doxorubicin, and it is the informative case.** Its gap widens 0.110 → 0.170 at matched dose — the far quartile's coverage drops from 0.889 to 0.829 while the near quartile stays at 0.999. Doxorubicin is the drug whose L is shortest and most changed by the fix (§5), so the smooth source was specifically flattering the drug most vulnerable to geometry. **For a poorly-penetrating drug, delivery does start to bind under the discrete field**, and any conclusion about such a drug should be re-checked against arm C.

But the general statement holds, and §4 explains why: on a section whose most distant tumour-cell quartile sits a median 82 µm from an endothelial cell, 6 h of diffusion erases most of the difference **regardless of how the source is drawn**. The conclusion was never resting mainly on the KDE; it rests on this section being densely and near-uniformly vascularised — which `vascular_validation.md` §4 already identified as the reconstruction's real deficiency ("to within a few percent, a random point field").

## 8. Should the discrete field become the default?

**Recommendation: NOT YET as the global default. Adopt it now as the REQUIRED field for any distance-resolved or perivascular claim, and keep the KDE as the default for the headline delivery metrics until the four items in §8.2 are done.**

This is a split verdict because the evidence is split, and collapsing it either way would be dishonest.

### 8.1 Where it genuinely changes the answer — adopt it there today

1. **It removes the source-null confound completely, not partially.** Under A the source itself fits a decay length (36.6 µm on the common axis, 21.3 µm on the legacy axis), so a drug's L is always partly the source's. Under B/C the source **admits no fit at all** — it is a step function. Any claim of the form "exposure decays with distance from a vessel with length L" is uninterpretable under A and clean under B. This is not a matter of degree.
2. **It roughly doubles cross-drug separation in L** (3.12× → 6.78× at matched dose) and the change is pure geometry: L is scale-invariant, and arms B and C — which differ only in dose — give L within 2.7% of each other.
3. **It ends the circularity in `niche_response.md`.** The perivascular *label* and the drug *source* no longer share the smoothed endothelial signal; the source is a step function of segmented objects, the label is not.
4. **It is the only vascular construction this project has that scores against a measured area fraction** (§1.2, 2.50% vs 2.1 ± 0.3%, untuned). A max-normalised KDE cannot be scored that way at all.

### 8.2 Why it should not yet be the global default

1. **The headline metric does not move.** `therapeutic_coverage @1× IC50` changes by at most **0.0166** at matched dose, against a ±0.03 grid-convergence band. Every absolute delivery conclusion in `results/` would be re-derived to reach the same number.
2. **The flat floor — the specific thing this was supposed to fix — survives** (25.99% → 23.18%, 1.12×), and §4 shows why: the diffusion and reaction-diffusion lengths are 1–2 orders of magnitude larger than the intervessel gap on this section. Switching the source does not buy the conclusion the audit expected.
3. **`alpha` was calibrated against the KDE field.** The perivascular-sink constant is the one fitted parameter every absolute exposure in this project inherits, and A and B differ 3.36× in total delivered drug with neither having an absolute scale. Flipping the default without re-fitting alpha silently rescales every AUC in `results/`. **This must be done first, not concurrently.**
4. **Grid convergence is untested and is the real risk.** A 7.5 µm vessel radius on a 12 µm mesh is not resolved — one grid cell already has an equivalent radius of 6.8 µm. The discrete field is inherently more grid-sensitive than a 40 µm-bandwidth KDE, and this study is single-`dx` **by design**. Until `numerical_rigor` is re-run on the discrete field, adopting it would trade a documented confound for an undocumented one.
5. **The deeper defect is upstream of the field and this does not touch it.** §1.2: the segmented vessels are still 0.97× a random point field on the median and 1.73× on the >100 µm tail. GBM's vascular hotspots and avascular zones are not present in the endothelial cell positions this section provides, so **no source-field construction can recover them**. Sharpening the field while the point pattern is structureless is polishing the wrong layer — and it is the point pattern, not the field, that would make delivery binding.

### 8.3 How to use it, concretely

```python
from tmesim.reconstruct.real_fields import vascular_density_from_cells
# default, unchanged:
kde   = vascular_density_from_cells(domain, cells)
# discrete, opt-in:
disc  = vascular_density_from_cells(domain, cells, mode='discrete')
```

`mode=` is an additive keyword; the KDE path is byte-for-byte unchanged and remains the default. It also forwards `link_distance_um`, `radius_um`, `amplitude` and `match_total_to`, so the dose-controlled arm C is one call.

It is also **the right substrate for the outstanding BBB-disruption leak** (`SESSION_AUDIT.md` §1: `source = vascular_density × (bbb_penetrance + L·bbb_disruption) × plasma`, L ≈ 0.24). An additive leak is a *per-vessel* property — a vessel is either disrupted or it is not — and a per-vessel property needs vessel objects. Under the KDE it can only be applied as a smooth multiplier on a blur. **If that fix is implemented, it should be implemented on the discrete field**, and that is a stronger argument for the discrete field than anything measured in this report.

### 8.4 If it is ever made the global default, these must be re-run

In dependency order — everything here reads `vascular_density` directly or through the O₂ / IFP solve:

1. `validation/calibration.py` — **first**: `alpha` is fitted against the KDE source, and every absolute exposure inherits it.
2. `validation/delivery_metrics.py` — coverage, penetration length, the grid-convergence and discrimination tables.
3. `validation/vessel_profile.py` — its vessel mask should become the segmented mask rather than a percentile on the field (`vascular_validation.md` §7.2 already asks for this).
4. `validation/numerical_rigor.py` — grid convergence must be re-established: a 7.5 µm feature on a 12 µm mesh is *not* resolved, so the discrete field is inherently more grid-sensitive than the KDE. **This is the single biggest risk of the switch and it is untested here** (this study is single-`dx` by design).
5. `validation/pharmacology_real.py`, `glioma_regional_pk.py`, `pk_multidrug.py` — the external validations.
6. `validation/uncertainty.py`, `ranking_robustness.py` — the drug ranking and its error bars.
7. `viz/patient_report.py` and every figure in `results/`.

## 9. What this does NOT establish

* **One section, one patient, one `dx`.** Between-case GBM vascularity spans ~48× (PMID 38928515). A section that was genuinely heterogeneous — with real avascular zones — could give the opposite answer, and that is the most important untested case. This study cannot distinguish "the discrete field does not matter" from "the discrete field does not matter *on a section that has no avascular zones*". The second is what the evidence actually supports.
* **Grid convergence of the discrete field is untested.** Single `dx` by design. See §8 item 4.
* **The vessel radius and the linking distance are free parameters.** Both are cited rather than fitted, and both are swept or scored (§1.3, §1.2), but neither is measured on this tissue.
* **Endothelial identity is a PECAM1 z-score gate.** Move the gate and MVD moves with it (`vascular_validation.md` §6). The segmentation inherits that entirely.
* **Nothing here validates the field's VALUES.** Both fields are relative. The area fraction in §1.2 validates the discrete field's *geometry* against a measurement; its *amplitude* remains unvalidated, as A's always was.
* **Arm C is hyperoxic.** Matching the total drug also scales the O₂ and IFP source (§2). Coverage and the exposure profile are insensitive to this (neither depends on the oxygen field), but the kill fraction is not, which is why kill fraction is not a headline here.
* **The 6 h exposure window is a modelling choice**, and the floor result is partly conditional on it: a much shorter window would leave less time for diffusion to fill the avascular gaps and the two fields would look more different. It is **not** simply a transient artefact, though — the *steady-state* reaction-diffusion length `L_rd` in §4 is 2–7× the intervessel gap on its own, so the floor would survive at infinite time too. The window is not swept here.
* **The `L` values in §5 are far longer than the isolated-single-vessel control** in `results/vessel_profile.md` would suggest, on both arms. They are section-scale statistics of a densely vascularised tissue, not single-vessel gradients, and the two should not be quoted interchangeably.
