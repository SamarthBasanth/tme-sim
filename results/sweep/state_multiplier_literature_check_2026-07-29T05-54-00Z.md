# Is there a literature basis for the `hypoxia_resistance = 0.35` / `quiescent_resistance = 0.5` VALUES themselves?

**Status: research only. No code changed.** This is a narrow, targeted follow-up, sibling to `results/oxygen_calibration_landscape.md` (which asked whether a real mmHg reference exists for the oxygen field) and to whatever concurrent work is fixing the oxygen sink term's functional form. Neither of those questions is repeated here. The question here is specific: independent of getting the oxygen field itself right, is there a real measurement in the radiobiology/pharmacology literature that supports the *numeric values* `0.35` (hypoxic) and `0.5` (quiescent) as chemotherapy kill-probability multipliers — as opposed to the well-known radiotherapy OER (~2.5–3, a different ratio for a different modality, deliberately excluded from this search)?

## Bottom line, stated plainly

**No direct calibration exists, and the closest real numbers argue the values are probably too severe for temozolomide specifically, while being roughly the right order of magnitude for a generic hypoxia effect on other cytotoxics.** This search found real, GBM-cell-line-specific, hypoxia-vs-normoxia chemosensitivity data — better data than the previous oxygen-field pass found for the resistance question, which came up completely empty. But every study that measured temozolomide (TMZ) directly reports a **much smaller** hypoxia effect than a 0.35 kill-probability multiplier implies, and one study found **no significant effect at all** in the exact 2D, matched-oxygen-only comparison that is structurally closest to what `tmesim/cells/pkpd.py` does. The `quiescent_resistance = 0.5` side is worse: no quantitative measurement of any kind was found — only a well-established mechanistic argument (cell-cycle-dependent drugs preferentially kill cycling cells) that supports the *direction* (< 1) but says nothing about the *magnitude* (0.5 vs 0.05 vs 0.9). This is a promising area for a future calibration pass in the narrow sense that real, extractable numbers exist for the hypoxia side — but the honest reading of those numbers is closer to "the current value looks too resistant for TMZ" than "the current value is validated."

## What was found, by drug — direct hypoxia-vs-normoxia measurements in GBM cell lines

### Temozolomide (this project's flagship drug) — the strongest, most on-topic evidence, and it argues the multiplier is too severe

Three real, cell-line-based, hypoxia-controlled studies were found. All three use GBM lines overlapping this project's own drug library (`temozolomide-like` in `tmesim/core/config.py:108-110`), and two of the three use the *same* three GBM lines (U87, U251, SNB19) already cited elsewhere in this project (`results/state_validation.md`, `kill_calibration.py` §"3-D:2-D test") via PMID 30694423 — for a different purpose (a 3D-vs-2D spheroid geometry bound, not the hypoxia question). Re-reading that same paper for *this* question surfaces a finding the project has not yet used:

1. **Musah-Eroje & Watson 2019** ("A novel 3D in vitro model of glioblastoma reveals resistance to temozolomide which was potentiated by hypoxia," *J Neurooncol* 142(2):231-240). PMID [30694423](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30694423/), PMC [6449313](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6449313/), DOI [10.1007/s11060-019-03107-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11060-019-03107-0). U87, U251, SNB19 GBM lines; 2D monolayer vs. 3D Cultrex basement-membrane culture; 20% O2 (normoxia) vs. 1% O2 (hypoxia); TMZ IC50 by Alamar Blue. **The headline "up to fivefold" resistance figure this project already cites is a 3D-vs-2D geometry effect, not a hypoxia effect.** The paper's own, separate, matched-oxygen-only comparison states directly: *"when glioblastoma cells were cultured under a hypoxic environment in the 2D model, there was no significant difference in temozolomide resistance when compared with cells cultured in the same 2D model but under normoxic condition."* A follow-up experiment (pre-exposing U251 cells to hypoxia for 24h before treatment, with one arm returned to normoxia) likewise found hypoxia produced **"no significant change to the resistance of temozolomide."** This is a real, direct, negative result in exactly the geometry-free comparison structurally closest to what `pkpd.py`'s multiplicative `R_hypoxia` represents (a well-mixed 2D/point comparison, not a 3D penetration effect that tme-sim already models separately via its transport PDE).

2. **Wang, Wan, Xiong et al. 2017** ("HIF1α regulates glioma chemosensitivity through the transformation between differentiation and dedifferentiation in various oxygen levels," *Sci Rep* 7(1):7965). PMID [28801626](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28801626/), PMC [5554160](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5554160/), DOI [10.1038/s41598-017-06086-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-06086-2). GL261 and U87 glioma cells, CD133-/CD15-/NESTIN- (differentiated) sorted populations, IC50 by CCK-8 at 250-2000 µM TMZ, compared across 1% O2 (hypoxia), 21% O2 (normoxia) and 95% O2 (hyperoxia). This paper reports the actual numbers, and they are small: relative to the 95% O2 baseline, GL261 IC50 was **1.2-fold higher at 21% O2 and 1.4-fold higher at 1% O2**; U87 IC50 was **1.1-fold higher at 21% O2 and 1.2-fold higher at 1% O2**. Isolating the hypoxia-vs-normoxia step specifically (1% O2 vs. 21% O2, dividing out the shared 95% O2 baseline): **GL261 ≈ 1.4/1.2 ≈ 1.17-fold, U87 ≈ 1.2/1.1 ≈ 1.09-fold** — a roughly 9-17% IC50 increase under hypoxia, described in the paper's own text as a difference that "was significant" only for the 21%-vs-1% comparison, not as a large effect. A second measurement in the same paper (U87 cells hypoxia-pre-treated then IC50-tested) gives **1.3-fold**, the largest hypoxia-specific TMZ number found across any source in this search.

3. **Ahmed, Bandopadhyay, Coyle, Grabowska 2018** ("A HIF-independent, CD133-mediated mechanism of cisplatin resistance in glioblastoma cells," *Cell Oncol (Dordr)* 41(3):319-328). PMID [29492900](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29492900/), PMC [5951876](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5951876/), DOI [10.1007/s13402-018-0374-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13402-018-0374-8). Same three lines as (1) — U251, U87, SNB19 — 2D and 3D, 20% vs. 1% O2, IC50 by Alamar Blue for **cisplatin, temozolomide, and etoposide** (two of this project's three drugs, directly). The paper reports **"increases in temozolomide resistance in the U87 and SNB19 cells, but these increases were not statistically significant."** The exact fold values are in a supplementary table not extractable from the full text retrieved in this pass (**UNVERIFIED to the decimal**), but the qualitative finding — real, GBM-specific, non-significant TMZ shift under hypoxia in two of three lines — corroborates (1) and (2): **whatever the true TMZ hypoxia effect is, multiple independent GBM cell-line studies converge on it being small, often statistically indistinguishable from zero, and nowhere near the ~2.9x-equivalent implied by a kill-probability multiplier of 0.35** (see "Metric mismatch," below, for why "2.9x" is only an approximate translation).

**Verdict for TMZ: real data exists, it is GBM-specific, and it says the current 0.35 multiplier is very likely too severe** — the honest reading of three independent studies is a hypoxia-specific TMZ resistance effect in the range of **~1.0x (not significant) to ~1.4x**, not the much larger effective resistance a 0.35 kill-probability multiplier implies at typical exposure levels.

### Cisplatin — real GBM data exists, direction confirmed, magnitude unquantified in this pass

The same Ahmed et al. 2018 paper (PMID 29492900, above) found a hypoxia-induced increase in cisplatin resistance in U251, U87 and SNB19 cells that **was** statistically significant in U251 and SNB19 (unlike TMZ). The exact fold value is in a supplementary table not extracted here — **UNVERIFIED to the decimal, but the direction and statistical significance are real and GBM-specific.** This is the one point in this search where hypoxia genuinely produced a *significant* chemoresistance shift for one of this project's three drugs in GBM cells specifically, so it is worth flagging as the best candidate for a follow-up pass that pulls the actual supplementary-table numbers.

### Etoposide — a real lower bound, but not one of tme-sim's three drugs

Same paper: **no IC50 could be obtained for etoposide under hypoxia even at the highest tested concentration (267 µM)**, versus a normoxic IC50 within range — the paper's own conclusion is "**>2-fold** resistant... under hypoxic conditions." This is a real, quantified (if only as a lower bound) GBM-specific hypoxia-chemoresistance number, and it is the single largest hypoxia-specific effect found in this search — but etoposide is not in tme-sim's `DRUG_LIBRARY` (`temozolomide-like`, `doxorubicin-like`, `cisplatin-like`), so it bears only indirectly on this project (as a plausibility ceiling: real hypoxia-chemoresistance effects for *some* cytotoxics in GBM cells can be large).

### Doxorubicin — a real, quantified fold-change, but wrong tissue

**Bell, Tse, Frederiksen, Gardhouse, Pang, Graham, Siemens 2007** ("Atrial natriuretic peptide attenuates hypoxia induced chemoresistance in prostate cancer cells," *J Urol* 177(2):751-756). PMID [17222675](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17222675/), DOI [10.1016/j.juro.2006.09.075](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2006.09.075). PC-3 and DU-145 human **prostate** carcinoma cells: 24h pre-incubation at 0.5% O2 (severe hypoxia) then 1-hour pulse exposure to 12.5 µM doxorubicin, clonogenic survival assay. Result, stated directly in the abstract: **"a subsequent 4 to 10-fold increase in their survival"** relative to normoxic (20% O2) controls (p < 0.001). This is a real, quantified, statistically robust fold-change for one of tme-sim's three drugs — but it is **prostate cancer, not glioma**, uses a short pulse-exposure protocol rather than the continuous-exposure AUC this project's `pkpd.py` models, and measures clonogenic *survival* rather than an IC50 shift (see "Metric mismatch" below for why survival-fold and kill-probability-multiplier are not the same number). It is the single closest-magnitude match to a 0.35-type multiplier found anywhere in this search (see next section), but only by analogy across tissue type and drug-exposure protocol, not as a direct GBM measurement.

## Quiescent_resistance = 0.5 — no numeric measurement of any kind found

This is the weaker half of the finding, and it should be stated as plainly as the strong half. Despite multiple search strategies (`"quiescent cell drug resistance factor"`, `"quiescence G0 arrest chemotherapy fold"`, `"serum starved quiescent cells cisplatin doxorubicin sensitivity"`, `"leukemia stem cell G0 quiescence chemotherapy survival fold cytarabine"`), **no study was found that reports a quantitative resistance ratio or fold-change between quiescent (G0-arrested) and cycling cancer cells for temozolomide, cisplatin, or doxorubicin, in any tissue.** What exists instead is a large, well-established *mechanistic* literature that supports the *direction* of the assumption but gives no magnitude:

- **Rohwer & Cramer 2011** ("Hypoxia-mediated drug resistance: novel insights on the functional interaction of HIFs and cell death pathways," *Drug Resist Updat* 14(3):191-201). PMID [21466972](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21466972/), DOI [10.1016/j.drup.2011.03.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drup.2011.03.001). States directly in the abstract: "Hypoxia inhibits tumor cell proliferation and induces cell cycle arrest, ultimately conferring chemoresistance since anticancer drugs preferentially target rapidly proliferating cells." This is a review-level mechanistic claim, not a measurement — it argues *why* a `< 1` multiplier for non-cycling cells is biologically reasonable, without quantifying it.
- **Musah-Eroje & Watson 2019** (above) makes the same mechanistic point specifically for TMZ in its Discussion: "TMZ depends on replication to convert O-methylguanine into lethal MMR-mediated lesions" — i.e., TMZ has a real, drug-specific, cell-cycle-dependence rationale, corroborated independently by the 2026 solamargine/TMZ paper found in this search (Ribeiro et al., PMID [41642473](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41642473/), PMC [13121190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13121190/), DOI [10.1007/s10637-026-01599-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10637-026-01599-y)), whose U-87MG mechanistic data show G2/M-arrested cells becoming *more* resistant to a co-administered agent because arrest removes cells from the S-phase window TMZ-MMR lethality depends on.
- **Wang et al. 2017** (above) shows GSCs (G0/G1-arrested, by their own flow cytometry) have "fewer apoptosis" after TMZ exposure than cycling differentiated cells — again directional, not quantified as a fold-change in the extracted text.
- No source found gives anything like "quiescent glioma cells survive TMZ N-fold better than cycling glioma cells at matched exposure" with an actual N.

**Verdict for quiescent_resistance: the direction (< 1) has strong mechanistic support; the specific value 0.5 has none.** This is a materially weaker position than the hypoxia side, where at least real numbers — even if they argue the current value is wrong — exist to argue with.

## Metric mismatch — why none of the above numbers can be plugged in directly, even the good ones

This section exists for the same reason `results/oxygen_calibration_landscape.md` §4 stopped short of the mmHg conversion: the honest thing to do with a real but not-directly-usable number is to say exactly why, not to round it into false precision.

`PDConfig.hypoxia_resistance` and `quiescent_resistance` are multipliers on **Hill kill *probability* at a given exposure** (`p_kill = p_hill * sensitivity * R`, `tmesim/cells/pkpd.py:36-43`). Every literature number found above is one of two different things:
1. An **IC50 fold-shift** (Wang 2017, Ahmed 2018 for cisplatin/TMZ, and implicitly the Musah-Eroje 2D null result) — i.e., "it takes N times more drug to produce the same effect." This is *not* the same quantity as "kill probability multiplied by R at fixed exposure." The translation between the two is nonlinear and depends on where on the Hill curve (`hill`, `ic50` from `DRUG_LIBRARY`) the comparison sits. As a rough illustration only (not a calibration): if a normoxic cell's kill probability at some exposure is `p_N` and IC50 shifts by a factor `f` under hypoxia, the hypoxic kill probability at the *same absolute exposure* becomes `p_H = p_N^h / (p_N^h + f^h*(1-p_N))`-type algebra specific to the Hill form — it is not simply `p_N / f`, and it is emphatically not a fixed ratio independent of exposure the way `R_hypoxia = 0.35` is written in `pkpd.py`. A ~1.1-1.4x IC50 shift (the TMZ numbers above) could correspond to anywhere from a negligible to a substantial kill-probability multiplier depending on how saturated the Hill curve is at the exposures tme-sim actually reaches — which `results/dose_schedule.md` and `results/state_validation.md` already document is *not* saturated in this project's typical runs.
2. A **clonogenic survival fold-change** (Bell 2007's 4-10x doxorubicin number) — closer in spirit to `(1-p_H)/(1-p_N)`, i.e., a ratio of *survival fractions*, not kill probabilities. This is the more directly comparable metric to a kill-probability multiplier, but even here the mapping is nonlinear and depends on the baseline kill probability `p_N`: at low `p_N` a 0.35 kill-multiplier produces a survival-fold-change close to 1 (negligible), while at high `p_N` (say 0.9) the same 0.35 multiplier produces a survival-fold-change around 6-7x — which happens to land inside Bell's reported 4-10x range, but only as a coincidence of algebra at one particular operating point, not a validation.

**The honest conclusion is that even the two best-matching numbers found in this pass — Wang 2017's ~1.1-1.4x TMZ IC50 shift and Bell 2007's 4-10x doxorubicin survival shift — cannot be converted into a validated value for `hypoxia_resistance` without first fixing an operating point (a representative baseline kill probability `p_N`) that this project's own exposure levels would need to supply.** That is a real, addressable next step (not a dead end), but it is a modeling decision, not a lookup — structurally the same kind of gap `results/oxygen_calibration_landscape.md` §4 identified for the oxygen-consumption constant.

## Is this a promising area for a future calibration pass, or a dead end?

**Promising, with an important caveat this pass makes concrete rather than hopeful.** Unlike the oxygen-consumption-rate search (`results/oxygen_calibration_landscape.md` §2, which came up completely empty for glioma), this search found:
- Three independent, GBM-cell-line-specific, hypoxia-vs-normoxia TMZ measurements (Musah-Eroje 2019, Wang 2017, Ahmed 2018), two of which report numbers directly, one of which reports a clean, well-controlled **null result** in the exact 2D geometry closest to `pkpd.py`'s implementation.
- One real, GBM-specific, statistically significant cisplatin resistance-under-hypoxia finding (Ahmed 2018) whose exact magnitude is one supplementary-table lookup away from being usable.
- One real, quantified, well-controlled doxorubicin fold-change (Bell 2007, 4-10x) that is at least directionally and drug-specifically relevant, if not tissue-matched.
- Zero quantitative anchors for `quiescent_resistance`, only mechanistic support for its direction.

**What this pass adds to the project's existing "unvalidated priors, no citation" verdict (`kill_calibration.py:1954`, `1997`) is not a citation to plug in, but a specific, falsifiable direction of concern: the best available GBM-specific TMZ evidence (1.0-1.4x IC50 shift, one outright null result) is far more modest than a 0.35 kill-probability multiplier implies for this project's flagship drug, while the cisplatin and doxorubicin evidence (real fold-changes, magnitude TBD or tissue-mismatched respectively) is less clearly inconsistent with it.** A future calibration pass has two concrete, tractable next steps: (1) pull the exact cisplatin/TMZ supplementary-table fold values from Ahmed et al. 2018 (PMC5951876's Electronic Supplementary Material, not retrieved in this pass) rather than relying on the paper's own "not statistically significant" / "significant" characterization; (2) decide the metric-mismatch operating point in "Metric mismatch" above before treating any IC50-fold or survival-fold number as directly substitutable for `hypoxia_resistance`. Neither step is a dead end — both are concrete, bounded pieces of work, unlike the oxygen-consumption search, which genuinely found nothing glioma-specific to build on.
