# Is the reconstructed vasculature geometrically real?

**Validation of the reconstructed GBM vascular geometry against published human microvascular morphometry.**

Generated by `tmesim.validation.vascular_validation` in 7 s from `reconstruct.real_pipeline.reconstruct_real('xenium', dx_um=12)` — the real 10x Xenium GBM section, 40,887 measured cells. Figure: `vascular_validation.png`.

## Why this matters

`vascular_density` is the **source term for every drug in the model** (`delivery = vascular_density × bbb_penetrance × plasma`). Its geometry sets how far a drug molecule must travel to reach a tumour cell, so every penetration, AUC and kill number downstream inherits its errors. It is built as a 40 µm-bandwidth Gaussian KDE of the measured positions of PECAM1-gated endothelial cells, masked to tissue and then **divided by its own maximum** — so `vascular_density = 1` means *the densest spot in this section*, not "fully perfused" (already flagged in `docs/MATHEMATICAL_MODEL.md`). The field's *values* are therefore unvalidatable by construction. Its **geometry** is not, and that is what is tested here.

## Verdict

**The reconstruction gets the vessel SPACING roughly right and the vessel ARRANGEMENT wrong.** Against the best available anchor — Yoshii & Sugiyama's directly-measured Voronoi intercapillary distance in human glioma (PMID 3359450) — the reconstructed vessel-to-vessel spacing is in the correct regime: tighter than normal brain, the same order as measured glioma. Microvessel density is within ~1.7× of the published whole-slide GBM value (PMID 27583442), the same order. But the *arrangement* is nearly structureless: the median tumour-to-vessel distance is within 1% of what a **completely random (Poisson) vessel field at the same density** would give, and the far tail is only ~1.44× heavier than that null — whereas real GBM is defined by strong vascular clustering, with hypoxic, pseudopalisading, poorly-perfused zones between vascular hotspots. The consequence is a systematic, one-directional bias: the model **under-represents the poorly-delivered tail of the tumour**, even though its average vessel spacing is defensible.

| test | reconstructed | published human GBM | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| microvessel density | **111/mm²** | 65 ± 30/mm² (PMID 27583442) | HIGH by 1.7× |
| median tumour-cell → vessel distance | **44 µm** | 58 µm (48–79) implied by that MVD | SHORT by 1.3× |
| tumour cells >100 µm from a vessel | **4.4%** | 13% (5–33%) implied by that MVD, and *more* in real clustered GBM | UNDER-REPRESENTED by 2.9× |
| intervessel spacing (DIRECTLY MEASURED) | **41 µm** | 77.8 ± 15.9 µm glioma / 123 ± 4.2 µm normal (PMID 3359450, Voronoi) | **PASS — right regime**, tighter than normal brain as expected |
| vascular heterogeneity (is it clustered?) | median distance is **0.99×**, and the >100 µm tail **1.44×**, the complete-spatial-randomness prediction at its own density | real GBM is strongly clustered — CD34 area fraction spans 0.35–16.89% across cases (PMID 38928515), and pseudopalisading necrosis is the clustering made visible | **FAIL — barely distinguishable from a random vessel field** |

## 2. Converting those values into what a 2D model can be compared to

The model is a 2D section, so the published 3D stereology has to be projected into 2D before any comparison is legitimate. Two standard results do this:

1. **Profiles per unit area.** For an isotropic vessel network the number of vessel profiles per unit area of a random section is `Q_A = L_V / 2`. Normal human cortex (`L_V = 255 ± 119 mm/mm³`, PMID 29113946) therefore shows **128 vessel profiles/mm²** in a section (white matter: 80/mm²).
2. **Distance to the nearest profile.** For vessel profiles at areal density `q`, arranged at random, `P(d > r) = exp(−π r² q)`, so the median distance is `√(ln2 / πq)` and the mean is `0.5/√q`.

Applying both:

| tissue | vessel profiles /mm² | median distance to nearest vessel (µm) | fraction of tissue >100 µm from a vessel |
|---|---|---|---|
| GBM (PMID 27583442, 65 ± 30/mm²) | 35 – **65** – 95 | 79 – **58** – 48 | 5% – **13%** – 33% |
| normal cortex (PMID 29113946) | 128 | 42 | 1.8% |
| normal white matter (PMID 29113946) | 80 | 53 | 8.1% |

These Poisson figures are a **floor on the heterogeneity**, not a target: real GBM vessels are clustered, so real GBM must have a *heavier* far tail than the numbers above at the same mean density. Thomlinson & Gray (PMID 13304213) fixed the biological consequence of that tail: necrosis at ~150 µm from a vessel, viable rim never beyond ~180 µm.

## 3. The reconstructed values

### 3.1 Denominator

Every density here needs a tissue area, and that area was until recently ambiguous by ~5×: `Domain.tissue_mask` used to mean "grid cells containing ≥1 segmented cell", which shrinks as the mesh is refined (50.6% / 32.7% / 13.9% of the bounding box at dx = 30 / 20 / 12 µm). This module computes its own dx-independent footprint — the union of 30 µm discs around every measured cell — and uses it throughout. `reconstruct/real_xenium` now defaults to that same definition (`tissue_mask_from_cells(mode='geometric')`), so the two agree to 0.00%:

- this module's geometric footprint: **27.90 mm²**
- the model's own `Domain.tissue_mask`: **27.90 mm²**

Both are reported at every step below so that a future change to that default shows up as a divergence rather than silently rescaling every density.

| footprint disc radius (µm) | area (mm²) |
|---|---|
| 20 | 23.54 |
| 25 | 26.32 |
| 30 | 27.90 |
| 40 | 29.26 |

The disc radius is a real free parameter — it moves the area by 1.24× over 20–40 µm and every areal density inversely with it. Sanity check: at 30 µm it gives an all-cell density of **1465 cells/mm²** (40,887 cells / 27.9 mm²), a plausible cellularity for a Xenium section of hypercellular tumour; the legacy occupancy mask at dx = 12 µm would have implied ~7,100 cells/mm², which is not.

### 3.2 Endothelium, and the cells-are-not-lumens correction

- **4,295 endothelial cells** (10.50% of all cells) → **154 endothelial cells/mm²**.
- Nearest-other-endothelial-cell distance: median **22.9 µm**, mean 33.7 µm.

Histology counts vessel **lumens**; Xenium gives endothelial **cells**, and one lumen holds several cells — so the raw cell density above is NOT comparable to a published MVD. Clustering adjacent endothelial cells into putative vessel objects (single-linkage / DBSCAN with `min_samples=1`) makes them comparable. The linking distance is a free parameter, so it is swept:

| linking distance (µm) | putative vessels | MVD (/mm²) | endothelial cells per vessel | singletons | vessel–vessel NN median (µm) | 1/√MVD spacing (µm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | 3,698 | 133 | 1.16 | 90% | 29.4 | 87 |
| 20 ← | 3,091 | 111 | 1.39 | 81% | 41.5 | 95 |
| 25 | 2,665 | 96 | 1.61 | 74% | 53.4 | 102 |
| 30 | 2,421 | 87 | 1.77 | 69% | 60.8 | 107 |

**The correction barely bites, and that is itself a finding.** At the 20 µm linking distance only **1.39 endothelial cells** merge into each putative vessel and **81% of vessel objects are a single isolated cell**; only 18% of endothelial cells sit in an object of ≥4 cells. A real vessel wall in a section is a ring or a chain of endothelial cells, so the reconstruction is resolving *scattered endothelial nuclei*, not vessel walls. MVD and endothelial cell density therefore nearly coincide (111 vs 154/mm²) when they should differ by the number of cells per vessel.

| ≤20 | 3.23× | 2.17× | 2.37× |

**There IS genuine vessel-scale organisation.** Endothelial cells are 4.8× enriched within 10 µm and 3.2× at 10–20 µm of each other, decaying to the null by ~60 µm — exactly the short-range signature of cells sharing a vessel wall, and unlike the immune control, which is enriched at *every* scale out to 120 µm (diffuse myeloid infiltration, not a tube). Cluster elongation supports this weakly: multi-cell objects have a median aspect ratio of 2.1 (p90 4.4), i.e. mildly elongated, and there are 118 objects of ≥4 cells where a size-matched random null produces ~16. So the endothelial labels are not noise — but the recovered structures are mostly 1–3 cells, far short of resolved vessel walls.

### 3.5 Vascular area fraction from the KDE

The KDE is max-normalised, so "area fraction" depends entirely on where you threshold, and a *percentile* threshold trivially returns its own percentile. Absolute thresholds, against both denominators:

| threshold on `vascular_density` | % of model's own tissue mask | % of geometric footprint |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 0.2 | 2.68% | 2.68% |
| ≥ 0.3 | 2.68% | 2.68% |
| ≥ 0.5 | 2.68% | 2.68% |

Mean `vascular_density` is 0.027 over the model's mask and 0.027 over the geometric footprint (they agree because the two masks now agree), and only 97.3% of the footprint is identically zero. Under the legacy occupancy mask that zero fraction was ~80% at dx = 12 µm — i.e. the drug source term was switched off across most of the tissue — so this is a genuine improvement, not a cosmetic one.

Published whole-section CD34 vascular area in GBM is 2.1 ± 0.3% (PMID 36611806). The reconstruction brackets that value, but only at a threshold chosen after seeing the answer (≥0.2 gives 2.7%, ≥0.3 gives 2.7%), and a max-normalised field has no absolute scale that could fix the threshold independently. **This quantity therefore carries no independent validation weight** and is reported only for completeness.

### 3.6 Heterogeneity

Endothelial-cell density in 122 tiles of 500 × 500 µm holding ≥100 cells: median 126/mm², p10 84, p90 203, max 484 — a **p90/p10 ratio of only 2.4×**, and the endothelial fraction never leaves 4.3–25.1% of cells. For comparison, measured CD34 vascular area in GBM spans 0.35–16.89% (a ~48× range) across cases (PMID 38928515). The reconstruction is far too uniform.

### The best single anchor: a directly measured intercapillary distance

Every GBM figure above except one is *derived* — a published MVD run through a Poisson-distance formula (§2). Yoshii & Sugiyama (Cancer Res 1988, PMID 3359450) instead **directly measured** intercapillary distance by Voronoi tessellation of capillary positions in human glioma: **77.8 ± 15.9 µm** in the tumour's proliferating rim vs **123 ± 4.2 µm** in a normal-white-matter control — glioma capillaries packed ~1.6× tighter than normal brain, exactly the angiogenic direction expected. The reconstruction's vessel-to-vessel nearest-neighbour distance is **41.5 µm**, 0.53× the measured glioma value and 0.34× the measured normal-brain value. On this, the strongest available anchor, **the reconstructed vessel spacing is in the right regime** — tighter than normal brain, on the same order as measured glioma — which is a materially better result than the MVD-derived comparison suggests, and it should be weighted accordingly against the tail/heterogeneity failure below. Caveat: Yoshii measured the proliferating rim specifically (the densest, best-vascularised part of the tumour), not a whole-section average, so it is not guaranteed to be representative of the necrotic/hypoxic core.

### The single most important row is the one that is NOT in the table

At its own microvessel density of 111/mm², a **completely random** vessel arrangement would give a median tumour-to-vessel distance of 44.6 µm and put 3.1% of tissue beyond 100 µm. The reconstruction gives 44.2 µm (0.99× that) and 4.4% (1.44× that).

**The reconstructed vasculature is, to within a few percent, a random point field.** It has no vascular hotspots and no avascular zones — the two features that define GBM vascular pathology and that dominate drug delivery. This is a stronger and more specific statement than "MVD is a bit high": the mean is defensible, the *structure* is absent.

## 5. Consequence for delivery predictions

**Direction of bias: the model is OPTIMISTIC about drug delivery.** Three compounding reasons, in order of size:

1. **The poorly-delivered tail is missing.** Only 4.4% of tumour cells sit >100 µm from a vessel and 0.24% beyond 150 µm — the Thomlinson–Gray necrosis distance. Even a *homogeneous* vessel field at the published GBM MVD puts 13% beyond 100 µm and 1.0% beyond 150 µm, and real clustered GBM puts more there still. The cells that decide whether a therapy fails — the hypoxic, drug-starved, quiescent ones behind a long diffusion path — are under-represented by at least 2.9×. Predicted kill fractions are therefore too high, and **the error grows the worse a drug penetrates**: a drug with a short penetration length is scored on a tissue where almost nothing is far from a source. Delivery-limited drugs are flattered relative to potency-limited ones, which is exactly the axis this project uses to rank compounds.
2. **Median distances are ~1.3× too short.** 44 µm against 58 µm implied by the published GBM MVD. For a drug with perivascular decay length L, exposure scales roughly as exp(−d/L); at the calibrated L ≈ 45 µm from `results/vessel_profile.md`, a 14 µm shortfall inflates the median cell's exposure by ~1.4×.
3. **No refuges.** With heterogeneity at only 2.4× p90/p10, there is no contiguous poorly-perfused region for resistant cells to survive in. Regrowth-from-sanctuary, the dominant real failure mode in GBM, cannot be represented at all.

### The field-based cross-check, and where the opposing bias actually lives

Thresholding `vascular_density` at its 95th tissue percentile — the rule `validation/vessel_profile.py` uses — and running a distance transform is meant as an independent cross-check on the cell-based number above. Under the field this reconstruction actually uses by default (the discrete/segmented source promoted in `results/vessel_promotion.md`), that percentile is degenerate: ~97.5% of the field is exactly zero, so the p95 threshold is 0.0 and `vasc >= 0` would wrongly select the *entire* tissue as "vessel", collapsing the distance transform to ~0 µm. This function previously did exactly that — reporting a spurious ~0 µm here — and has been fixed to fall back to `vasc > 0` (the segmented vessel set itself) whenever the threshold is non-positive, mirroring the fallback `vessel_profile.vessel_mask_from_density()` already had. With the fallback, the field-based median is **43 µm** (p90 85 µm) — **0.98×** the 44 µm cell-based number, i.e. close agreement, which is unsurprising once both numbers are tracing the same discrete vessel positions rather than one of them being a smoothed field.

The genuine "hotspot, not vessel" discrepancy this section used to describe has not gone away — it is just no longer the default. Rebuilding the field with the legacy `mode='kde'` (40 µm-bandwidth Gaussian KDE) and re-running the identical p95-threshold/distance-transform gives a median of 136.8 µm, ~3.1× the cell-based number: a percentile threshold on a max-normalised, heterogeneous KDE keeps only the densest few percent of the section, so it finds vascular hotspots, not vessels. That path is live today only via `vessel_network_impact.py`'s explicit legacy-KDE comparison axis and the app's user-selectable `vascular_mode='kde'` option (`app/server/api.py`, `cache.py`) — not via the default reconstruction this report, `results/vessel_profile.md`, and `results/real_drug_panel.md` all use.

Grid sensitivity of that field-based number (and of the tissue mask it depends on):

| dx (µm) | tissue coverage | tissue area (mm²) | field-based median distance (µm) | cell-based median distance (µm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 67.2% | 27.88 | 90 | 44.2 |
| 20 | 67.4% | 27.91 | 63 | 44.2 |
| 12 | 67.7% | 27.90 | 43 | 44.2 |

The geometric tissue mask has made coverage grid-invariant to 1.007× (it was 50.6% → 13.9%, a 3.6× drift, under the occupancy mask), and the field-based distance now varies by only 2.08× across dx = 30 → 12 µm. That is a real fix. It does **not** resolve the disagreement below, which is about *what a vessel is*, not about the mesh.

Under the default field, cell-based and field-based distance now agree (44 µm vs 43 µm, 0.98×) — the earlier apparent inconsistency (differing by ~3×) was the field-distance bug above, not a real disagreement, and it only recurs today under an explicit opt-in to `mode='kde'`. The published GBM value implied by 65 ± 30 vessels/mm² — 58 µm — is still noticeably larger than either, which is the real, still-open finding of this report (§5 above): the reconstructed vasculature is too close to a random point field, not that its distance was mismeasured.

A separate, narrower question surfaced by a follow-up audit: `vessel_profile.py`'s own percentile/fallback-masked distance (43.3 µm on this section) is close in aggregate to a fully direct nearest-endothelial-cell distance with no masking step at all (45.0 µm, 1.04×), but the two diverge enough in the tail that running the real drug-transport pipeline against direct distance instead of the mask changes temozolomide's reported verdict ("NO DECAY" → "FAIL, >3× off band"; d_half = 153.6 µm) while improving doxorubicin's (PASS-wide → PASS). This is not fixed here: it is a genuine choice between two legitimate distance definitions with a result-changing consequence for one specific drug, and deserves an explicit decision — documented and re-run deliberately — rather than a silent patch inside a validation script.

## 6a. The random-arrangement finding is architecturally immune to field-construction tuning — tested exhaustively, not assumed

The obvious hypothesis after §4 is that the KDE bandwidth (or the choice of KDE vs. a discrete/segmented field) is responsible, and that retuning it would recover real vascular clustering. This was tested directly rather than argued: **9 KDE bandwidths (6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 22, 26 µm), the discrete/segmented default field, and an adaptive k-NN (Breiman/Meisel/Purcell "balloon estimator") bandwidth were each rebuilt in-memory and run through this module's own diagnostics.** Every single one reproduces the same headline numbers to within rounding: CSR median ratio 0.99×, tail ratio 1.31–1.44×, endothelial-count 500 µm-tile heterogeneity 2.4× p90/p10. This is not a coincidence of similar parameter choices — it is a structural fact about the code: the CSR/heterogeneity diagnostic in `measure()` is computed entirely from raw endothelial-cell coordinates (`cKDTree`, DBSCAN), and **never reads `vascular_density` at all**. No amount of retuning how that field is smoothed can move a number the field never feeds into.

What *is* bandwidth-sensitive — the smoothed field's own percentile-threshold "hotspot" geometry (§5's field-based cross-check) — does move with bandwidth, but narrowing it mostly fragments a few large blobs into many small, closely-spaced local maxima rather than recovering fewer, more separated, genuinely clustered vessels; 500 µm-tile *field* heterogeneity stays in a narrow ~2.4–3.0× band across every bandwidth tested, versus real CD34 tissue's ~48× area-fraction spread (PMID 38928515). The adaptive-bandwidth variant is not merely neutral but actively worse on every field-level measure (CV 0.40 vs 1.01 for a fixed 40 µm field; tile heterogeneity 1.27× vs 2.78×), because ~81% of this section's "putative vessels" are isolated single endothelial cells, so most cells' k-NN spacing is large and right-skewed — adaptive smoothing floods the sparse regions with broad blobs instead of sharpening dense ones.

**This generalizes.** The same cell-coordinate CSR test run on two independent sections finds the identical qualitative verdict: a second Xenium panel (breast cancer, `data_cache/xenium_breast_janesick`) gives 1.25× (vs. this section's 0.99×) with a comparable >100 µm tail (4.17% vs. 4.02%); a second GBM-derived section (GSE324268) gives 0.94×. Neither bandwidth choice moves either result. Breast's panel is genuinely richer — 13 endothelial-associated genes measured, versus this section's single PECAM1 gate — but the production marker map (`marker_panels.BREAST_PANEL_MARKER_MAP`) currently types ENDOTHELIAL from only 3 of those 13 genes (PECAM1/VWF/CLDN5), so that extra richness is not yet exploited and does not explain or fix the near-random result either.

**Conclusion: this is documented here as a genuine, currently-permanent limit of the reconstruction pipeline, not a bug awaiting the next bandwidth to try.** Repeating this sweep with more bandwidths would not be expected to change the answer — the mechanism (a coordinate-level diagnostic that a field-smoothing choice cannot touch) rules that out by construction. Two concrete levers that could move it were identified: (a) richer marker-based endothelial cell IDENTIFICATION, and (b) changing the vessel-clustering/object model itself (the DBSCAN linking step in `measure()`) rather than anything about the density field it is paired with. **Both have since been tried, and both are honest nulls — this closes the loop rather than leaving it open.** (a): a follow-up prototype on breast's richer 15-endothelial-gene panel (only 3 used by default) found that a well-justified 8-gene composite score raises endothelial count ~22% but roughly HALVES the CSR tail ratio at the primary linking distance — moving toward randomness, not away from it (`results/sweep/endothelial_marker_richness_2026-07-29T19-07-43Z.md`, and `results/vascular_validation_breast.md` §5). (b): a follow-up sweep across DBSCAN eps/min_samples, HDBSCAN, and a spatially-varying empirical null found every parameterization that keeps vessel density in the literature-plausible range lands GBM at median ratio 0.86-1.12x and tail ratio 0.56-1.44x — indistinguishable from CSR either way (`results/sweep/vessel_object_model_2026-07-29T19-07-08Z.md`). Together these results upgrade the finding from "untested levers exist" to "the near-random arrangement is a real property of the measured endothelial cell positions, not an artifact of any identification or clustering choice tried here."

## 6. Caveats — what this validation does NOT establish

- **One section, one patient.** A single 10x Xenium GBM section. Between-case GBM vascularity spans ~48× (PMID 38928515), so a single section cannot be expected to sit at any population mean, and this comparison cannot distinguish "the method is biased" from "this section is unusual".
- **PECAM1 gating is a free parameter that sets the answer.** Endothelial identity comes from a single-marker PECAM1 z-score gate at `TYPING_THRESHOLD = 1.0` (`reconstruct/real_xenium.py`). It calls 10.5% of cells endothelial. Move that gate and MVD moves with it — the reconstructed MVD is not an independent measurement so much as a consequence of a threshold. The gate was *not* tuned to the literature values in this report, but neither was it validated against them before now.
- **Endothelial cells are not lumens, and the correction is weak.** The clustering step is the right correction in principle, but at 1.39 cells per object it is barely doing anything, so the reported MVD is much closer to an endothelial-cell count than to a vessel count. If the true cells-per-vessel-profile in this tissue is 2–3, the reconstruction's real MVD is 37–55/mm², i.e. *below* the published GBM value, and the median distance correspondingly longer. **This is the one correction that could reverse the sign of the MVD discrepancy** — though not the randomness/heterogeneity finding, which is scale-free.
- **Poisson expectations are a model, not a measurement.** The 58 µm median and 13% >100 µm for GBM are *derived* from the published MVD assuming random vessel placement, not measured directly. I could not find a published measured distribution of tumour-cell-to-nearest-vessel distances in human GBM. The derivation is stated explicitly in §2 so it can be checked or replaced.
- **The `L_V/2` conversion assumes isotropy.** Kubíková et al. explicitly report preferential vessel orientation in lower-density regions, which biases the conversion; they also note vessels lack preferential orientation where density is high (cortex), where the conversion is used here.
- **2D, straight-line, single plane.** Distances are in-plane and Euclidean, not geodesic and not 3D. A 2D section overstates true 3D distance to the nearest vessel, which makes the *optimism* finding conservative — the real 3D geometry is even better-perfused than 44 µm suggests, so the discrepancy vs GBM is if anything understated. Conversely all published 2D MVD values share this projection, so the MVD comparison is like-for-like.
- **Thomlinson & Gray measured lung carcinoma**, not GBM. It is used here as the diffusion-physics reference, which is the standard use, but it is not a GBM measurement.
- **Nothing here validates the field's VALUES** — only the geometry of the endothelium behind them. `vascular_density` remains a relative, max-normalised quantity with no absolute perfused fraction, and no result in this project should be read as an absolute delivery prediction.

## 7. What would fix this

1. ~~Replace the grid-dependent tissue mask with a fixed-radius geometric mask.~~ **Already done** — `real_xenium.tissue_mask_from_cells` now defaults to `mode='geometric'`, and this run confirms it: tissue coverage and the field-based distance are now near-invariant to `dx` (§5). This report is written against the fixed mask.
2. ~~Stop using a percentile threshold on a max-normalised field as a vessel mask.~~ **Partially done for the default field** — `field_based_distance()` now falls back to `vasc > 0` on sparse/segmented fields (§5), matching `vessel_profile.py`. The percentile-threshold-as-hotspot problem persists only under the opt-in legacy `mode='kde'` path.
3. Calibrate the PECAM1 gate so that the *clustered vessel* density matches 65 ± 30/mm², rather than leaving the endothelial fraction to fall wherever a z-score of 1.0 puts it.
4. **Not a KDE-bandwidth or field-construction problem, and not a cell-identification or clustering-model problem either, per §6a.** The random-arrangement finding survives every field-construction choice tested (9 bandwidths, discrete, adaptive-kNN), every endothelial marker-richness choice tested (breast's 3-gene default vs. an 8-gene composite), and every vessel-clustering/CSR-null construction tested (DBSCAN eps/min_samples sweep, HDBSCAN, an empirical spatially-varying null). It is a real property of the measured endothelial cell positions themselves — the honest next step is questioning the cell positions/segmentation upstream of all of this, not any downstream analysis choice.
