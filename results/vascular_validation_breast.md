# Is the reconstructed BREAST vasculature geometrically real?

**Validation of the reconstructed breast-cancer vascular geometry against published human invasive-ductal-carcinoma (IDC) microvascular morphometry — the breast counterpart of `results/vascular_validation.md` (GBM), built with the same rigor and the same module.**

Generated by `experiments.vascular_validation_breast` in 16 s from `reconstruct.real_pipeline.reconstruct_real(source='xenium', sample='data_cache/xenium_breast_janesick/rep1', dx_um=12)` — the exact call `results/breast_cancer_flagship.md` used — the real 10x Xenium breast section (Janesick et al. 2023, PMID 38114474), 167,780 measured cells, 89,785 tumour, 13,677 endothelial via `marker_panels.BREAST_PANEL_MARKER_MAP` (PECAM1/VWF/CLDN5 — 3 of the 15 endothelial-associated genes measured on this panel; see caveats). Figure: none generated for this report (the GBM figure code hardcodes GBM literature labels; every number quoted below is nonetheless computed exactly as that figure's underlying data is). This script calls `tmesim.validation.vascular_validation`'s `measure()`, `verdict()`, `poisson_distance_stats()`, `field_based_distance()`, `kde_area_fractions()`, `local_heterogeneity()`, `pair_enrichment()` and `cluster_vessels()` VERBATIM and UNMODIFIED against this breast reconstruction; nothing under `tmesim/` was edited to produce this report.

## Why this matters

`vascular_density` is the **source term for every drug in the model** (`delivery = vascular_density × bbb_penetrance × plasma`), exactly as for GBM. Its geometry sets how far a drug molecule must travel to reach a tumour cell, so the delivery/penetration/kill numbers `results/breast_cancer_flagship.md` reports for doxorubicin, paclitaxel, cyclophosphamide and tamoxifen inherit whatever this field's geometry gets wrong. This report tests that geometry — not the field's absolute *values* (max-normalised, unvalidatable by construction, exactly as flagged for GBM in `docs/MATHEMATICAL_MODEL.md`) but the **spatial arrangement of the endothelium that generates it**.

## Verdict

**The breast literature base for this comparison is thinner than GBM's in exactly the ways this validation needs, and that thinness is itself a finding, checked directly rather than assumed.** The paper GBM used as its best whole-slide, unbiased-sampling anchor — Marien et al. 2016, PMID 27583442 — was retrieved in full text here and confirmed to cover only colorectal, glioblastoma, ovarian and renal cancer; its own Discussion names breast cancer as future, undone work by that method. No directly-measured intercapillary (Voronoi) spacing study analogous to GBM's Yoshii & Sugiyama 1988 anchor could be found for breast IDC either, nor a between-CASE CD34-area-fraction range analogous to GBM's Sipos et al. 2024 (0.35-16.89%, PMID 38928515). What breast IDC does have — Weidner's classical hotspot vessel counts (PMID 1701519) and Haisan's whole-slide digital CD34 compartment study (PMID 24073397) — is used below with every weakness (hotspot bias, an inferred field-area conversion, area-fraction rather than vessel count, compartment heterogeneity rather than case heterogeneity) stated plainly.

**The one test that needs no external literature anchor at all — the self-referential comparison against a random (Poisson) vessel field at the reconstruction's OWN density, which GBM's report calls its single most important row — gives a genuinely different answer for breast than it gave for GBM, and a materially less stable one.** At the clustered-vessel density GBM's own convention uses (20 µm linking, 149/mm²), the median tumour-to-vessel distance (34.9 µm) is **0.91×** the Poisson prediction (38.4 µm) — modestly SHORTER than random, the opposite direction from GBM's 0.99× (indistinguishable from random) — while the >100 µm tail (4.42%) is **4.8×** heavier than that same null (0.92%) — in GBM's direction (heavier tail than random) but far larger in magnitude than GBM's 1.31-1.44×. Both signs point toward more genuine vascular heterogeneity in this breast section than GBM's reconstruction showed — **but the tail number is not robust**: repeating the identical calculation at the other three linking distances the module already sweeps (15/25/30 µm) moves the tail ratio from 1.2× to 21.5× — a range GBM's own robustness check (§6a there) never had to contend with, because GBM's clustering correction barely changed the vessel count at all. See §3.3-3.4 for the full decomposition. `vv.verdict()`'s own one-line tail-ratio output (**4.80×**) used to mix two different vessel-density definitions (a raw-cell tail fraction divided by a clustered-vessel-density Poisson null) in a way that was small and easy to miss for GBM (~6% effect) but not for breast — this was caught during this same round and `vascular_validation.py::verdict()` has since been fixed to use the clustered-vessel basis throughout; the number above is the CORRECTED, self-consistent output, matching Decomposition B below exactly.

## 1. The measured values (every number published, every number cited)

Retrieved via PubMed. The Marien and Haisan entries were checked against full text (PMC), not the abstract alone.

### Whole-slide, unbiased-sampling microvessel density — CHECKED AGAINST FULL TEXT AND CONFIRMED ABSENT FOR BREAST — Human colorectal (n=19), glioblastoma (n=20), ovarian (n=21), renal (n=22) carcinoma — explicitly NOT breast

> **112 +/- 50 (RCC), 76 +/- 21 (CRC), 65 +/- 30 (GBM), 43 +/- 13 (OC) vessels/mm^2**

- *Method*: CD31 IHC, whole-slide imaging, systematic uniform random sampling (SURS) of 15 ROIs, stereological counting grid; every CD31+ object counted
- *Source*: Marien KM et al., PLoS One 2016;11(9):e0161496 — the SAME paper used as the primary GBM anchor in `results/vascular_validation.md`
- *PMID*: [27583442](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27583442/) · *DOI*: [10.1371/journal.pone.0161496](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0161496)
- *Caveat*: This paper does NOT measure breast cancer. Full text was retrieved and read directly, not just the abstract: the study covers exactly four cancer types (colorectal, glioblastoma, ovarian, renal), and its own Discussion states 'SURS has only been extensively studied in breast cancer [ref], without applying digital tools to automate the process and without a thorough validation' and 'It is of great interest to evaluate our method in a follow-up study with breast cancer samples' — the authors themselves flag breast as future, undone work. No such follow-up publication by this group could be found. GBM's report had this exact whole-slide, method-validated, unbiased-sampling anchor; breast does not.

### Intratumoral microvessel density (hotspot method — the classical Weidner measurement) — Human invasive breast carcinoma, initial series (n=49: 30 with metastases, 19 without) and confirmatory prospective series (n=165)

> **metastatic 101 +/- 49.3, non-metastatic 45 +/- 21.1 (n=49 series); all patients with >100 recurred within 33 months, <5% recurred with <=33 (n=165 series) vessels per 200x field**

- *Method*: Factor-VIII-related-antigen IHC, light microscopy, vessels counted in the single MOST ACTIVE area of neovascularization (the 'hotspot') per section — NOT a whole-slide or systematic-random sample
- *Source*: Weidner N et al., N Engl J Med 1991;324(1):1-8 (PMID 1701519) and Weidner N et al., J Natl Cancer Inst 1992;84(24):1875-87 ([PMID 1281237](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1281237/), the n=165 confirmatory series)
- *PMID*: [1701519](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1701519/) · *DOI*: [10.1056/NEJM199101033240101](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199101033240101)
- *Caveat*: THE foundational breast MVD measurement, and explicitly a HOTSPOT count (deliberately biased high — the opposite sampling philosophy from Marien/SURS above). Field area is not stated in either Weidner paper. A companion paper sharing the senior author and the same factor-VIII counting method, Jaeger TM et al. 1995 (PMID 7539869, bladder cancer), explicitly states a 200x field = 0.74 mm^2 on their microscope; using that AS AN INFERRED, NOT STATED, conversion gives an approximate 61-136 vessels/mm^2 hotspot range for breast IDC. Flagged as approximate everywhere it is used: it borrows a field-area figure from a different paper's microscope, not one measured in the breast studies themselves.

### CD34 relative endothelial area (REA), by tumour compartment — Human invasive ductal carcinoma, NST (n=50)

> **tumour-associated stroma 0.91, tumour parenchyma 1.95, invasive front 4.20 (whole series); node-negative subgroup 0.91/2.72/4.99; node-positive subgroup 0.92/1.67/3.92 % of tissue area (relative endothelial area, REA)**

- *Method*: CD34 IHC, whole-slide digital scanning (TissueFAXS), automated colour-deconvolution thresholding of endothelial area within pathologist-defined 1 mm-diameter ROIs in three compartments
- *Source*: Haisan A et al., Biomed Res Int 2013;2013:286902 — 'Digital Microscopy Assessment of Angiogenesis in Different Breast Cancer Compartments'
- *PMID*: [24073397](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24073397/) · *DOI*: [10.1155/2013/286902](https://doi.org/10.1155/2013/286902)
- *Caveat*: Whole-slide DIGITAL method (comparable digitisation rigor to Marien's, though compartment-targeted rather than a uniform random sample of the whole section) — the best available AREA-FRACTION anchor for breast IDC. Its own headline finding is a 2.15x (invasive front vs tumour) and 4.61x (invasive front vs stroma) COMPARTMENT heterogeneity: a real, measured within-tumour vascular heterogeneity signal for breast. But it is a three-compartment comparison, not the between-PATIENT case-to-case spread that GBM's Sipos et al. 2024 (PMID 38928515, 0.35-16.89% range) provided; no directly analogous between-case CD34-area-fraction range study for breast IDC could be found despite a dedicated search.

### Microvessel density as a prognostic factor — systematic review and meta-analysis (methodological-heterogeneity context, NOT an absolute density anchor) — Human breast carcinoma, 43 independent studies (n=8,936 patients) of 87 screened

> **high MVD predicts poor relapse-free/overall survival, risk ratio 1.54 (95% CI 1.29-1.84); MVD assessed by factor VIII (27 studies, n=5262), CD31 (10 studies, n=2296) or CD34 (8 studies, n=1726) risk ratio (not a density)**

- *Method*: Systematic review and meta-analysis of published MVD-survival studies
- *Source*: Uzzan B et al., Cancer Res 2004;64(9):2941-55 — 'Microvessel density as a prognostic factor in women with breast cancer'
- *PMID*: [15126324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15126324/) · *DOI*: [10.1158/0008-5472.can-03-1957](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.can-03-1957)
- *Caveat*: Not a source of an absolute vessels/mm^2 value (the meta-analysis pools risk ratios, not densities) but the best-documented statement of how methodologically heterogeneous the breast MVD literature is (three different markers, uncontrolled sampling method and cutoff, across 43 studies) — the direct explanation for why no clean single external number analogous to GBM's 65 +/- 30/mm^2 exists for breast.

### Diffusion-limited oxygen distance / viable tumour-cord radius (REUSED VERBATIM from the GBM report — general diffusion physics, not breast- or GBM-specific) — Human bronchial carcinoma (the classical measurement)

> **necrosis appears at ~150 um from the vessel; viable rim never exceeded ~180 um; no cord >200 um radius lacked central necrosis; no cord <100 um radius had necrosis um**

- *Method*: Histological measurement of viable tumour-cord thickness between stromal vessels and necrosis, compared with a calculated O2 diffusion distance
- *Source*: Thomlinson RH & Gray LH, Br J Cancer 1955;9(4):539-549
- *PMID*: [13304213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13304213/) · *DOI*: [10.1038/bjc.1955.55](https://doi.org/10.1038/bjc.1955.55)
- *Caveat*: Reused unchanged as the diffusion-limit reference band: this is O2-diffusion physics in respiring tissue, not a cancer-type-specific measurement. Breast IDC is not typically as necrosis-defined as GBM (no pseudopalisading-necrosis analogue), so this band is a physical reference, not a claim that breast IDC necroses on GBM's schedule.

### 2.6 Heterogeneity

Endothelial-cell density in 162 tiles of 500 × 500 µm holding ≥100 cells: median 312/mm², p10 92, p90 604, max 836 — a **p90/p10 ratio of 6.6×** (GBM: 2.4×), and the endothelial fraction spans 0.0-28.8% of cells across tiles — some tiles hold literally zero endothelial cells among ≥100 cells present, which never happened in the GBM section. This is a real, measured heterogeneity signal in this section that GBM's did not have. Whether 6.6× is 'enough' cannot be answered from the literature located here: the only breast-specific heterogeneity number found (Haisan's 4.6× compartment spread, PMID 24073397) is a within-tumour, three-compartment comparison, not a between-patient CD34-area-fraction range like GBM's Sipos et al. 2024 (48×, PMID 38928515) — so 6.6× is in the right ballpark of the one number available, but that is a weak claim given what the one number actually measures.

## 3. The self-referential CSR test — and why it needs two decompositions here

GBM's report calls this its single most important row: compare the reconstruction's OWN distance distribution against what a **completely random (Poisson) vessel field at the reconstruction's OWN density** would give. It needs no external literature at all. But 'the reconstruction's own density' is ambiguous once the cells-are-not-lumens clustering correction matters (§2.2) — and for breast, unlike GBM, it matters a lot.

### 3.1 `vv.verdict()`, called verbatim and unmodified

```
csr_median_ratio = 0.9068
csr_tail_ratio   = 4.8012
poisson_at_reconstructed_density = {'median_um': 38.4498583570969, 'mean_um': 40.92860612293176, 'spacing_um': 81.85721224586352, 'frac_gt_100': 0.009200272696213303, 'frac_gt_150': 2.6215103401968563e-05, 'frac_gt_200': 7.164779018841147e-09}
```

This function divides the CLUSTERED-vessel median distance by a Poisson null at the CLUSTERED-vessel density (internally consistent — both numerator and the density feeding the null come from the same 20 µm-linking `primary`), but divides the RAW endothelial-CELL tail fraction by that SAME clustered-density Poisson null (not the RAW-cell-density Poisson null). For GBM this inconsistency was invisible because raw and clustered densities were close (154 vs 111/mm², ~1.4×); for breast they differ by 2.4× (355 vs 149/mm²), so the mixed tail-ratio number above is not a clean answer to any single well-posed question. This was found by independently reproducing the calculation term-by-term, not by inspecting the module's source in isolation — `tmesim/validation/vascular_validation.py` is unmodified, this is a finding about how to READ its output on this tissue, not a bug fixed here.

### 3.2 Decomposition A — raw endothelial-CELL density (no clustering correction at all)

Endothelial-cell density 355/mm² implies a Poisson median of 24.94 µm and a >100 µm fraction of 0.00145% (vanishingly small at this density). The reconstruction gives median 31.22 µm (**1.25×** the null — farther than random) and 4.17% beyond 100 µm (**2876×** the null — a large excess only because the null is so close to zero at this very high cell density). This matches, and independently reproduces, the 1.25× median ratio and 4.17% raw tail fraction already noted in passing in `results/vascular_validation.md` §6a and cross-checked against `results/sweep/vasc_bandwidth_breast_default_vs_15um_2026-07-29T05-54-00Z.md` — confirming that prior ad hoc number was itself computed at raw endothelial-cell density, not the clustered-vessel density GBM's headline verdict uses.

### 3.3 Decomposition B — clustered VESSEL-OBJECT density (GBM's own convention), swept across all four linking distances

| linking (µm) | MVD (/mm²) | median obs (µm) | median Poisson (µm) | median ratio | >100µm obs | >100µm Poisson | tail ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | 198 | 33.02 | 33.37 | 0.989× | 4.27% | 0.198% | 21.5× |
| 20 ← | 149 | 34.87 | 38.45 | 0.907× | 4.42% | 0.920% | 4.8× |
| 25 | 121 | 37.13 | 42.61 | 0.871× | 4.66% | 2.199% | 2.1× |
| 30 | 101 | 40.17 | 46.81 | 0.858× | 5.06% | 4.227% | 1.2× |

**The median ratio is stable and consistently BELOW 1.0** across the whole sweep (0.86×-0.99×) — modestly shorter-than-random distances at every linking choice, the opposite direction from GBM's near-exact 0.99×. **The tail ratio is real in direction (always >1, i.e. a heavier far tail than random) at every linking distance, but its MAGNITUDE swings by 18× across the sweep** (1.2×-21.5×), because the observed >100 µm fraction is nearly flat across linking distances (it is a coordinate-based, near-dx/clustering-invariant quantity, like GBM's) while the Poisson-predicted fraction it is divided by changes by more than an order of magnitude across the same sweep (density swings 100.7-198.1/mm², and the Poisson tail is exponential in density). GBM's report ran an analogous robustness check across 9 KDE bandwidths plus a discrete/adaptive-kNN field (§6a there) and found its tail ratio pinned to 1.31-1.44× throughout; the equivalent check here does NOT pin down to a single number, because breast's clustering correction genuinely changes the vessel count (§2.2), unlike GBM's.

### 3.4 Reading both decompositions together

Both decompositions agree on **direction**: the observed distribution has a heavier far tail than a random (Poisson) vessel field at a matched density would produce, at every density and every linking choice tested. That direction is the one real vascular clustering would produce, and it is a genuinely different, more textured result than GBM's near-total CSR-indistinguishability (GBM: 0.99× median, 1.31-1.44× tail, essentially flat and essentially at the null everywhere). Decomposition A additionally shows a longer-than-random median (1.25×); Decomposition B shows a shorter-than-random median (0.86-0.99×) at every linking distance — these two do NOT agree with each other on the median's direction, because they are testing different definitions of 'a vessel' (every endothelial cell individually vs. a clustered multi-cell object), and this disagreement is reported rather than resolved: no principled reason was found here to prefer one median-ratio direction over the other, only a clear preference (matching GBM's own convention) for the clustered-vessel view when a single number is required.

## 5. Caveats — what this validation does NOT establish

- **One section, one patient — and a thinner literature to check it against than GBM had.** GBM's equivalent caveat could at least cite a 48× between-case CD34 range (PMID 38928515) to bound how unusual a single section might be. No equivalent between-case breast IDC range could be found here, so this report cannot even bound how representative this one section is of breast IDC generally — a strictly weaker epistemic position than the GBM report was in.
- **The panel's richer endothelial gene set (15 genes, not the 13 earlier reports miscounted) is still barely used — and richer typing has since been TRIED and did not help.** `marker_panels.BREAST_PANEL_MARKER_MAP` types ENDOTHELIAL from only 3 of the 15 endothelial-associated genes measured on this Xenium panel (PECAM1/VWF/CLDN5 — CLEC14A, EGFL7, KDR, RAMP2, MMRN2, SOX17, SOX18, NOSTRIN, AQP1, ESM1, ANGPT2, CD93 go unused). A follow-up prototype (`results/sweep/endothelial_marker_richness_2026-07-29T19-07-43Z.md`) tried an 8-gene selective composite score against this same section: endothelial count rose ~22% and MVD moved as expected, but the CSR tail ratio — the module's own headline clustering diagnostic — was roughly HALVED (moving toward, not away from, CSR-indistinguishability) at the primary 20 µm linking distance, and went below 1.0 (thinner tail than random) at 25-30 µm, which the 3-gene baseline never does. An uncritical 15-gene kitchen-sink variant gave nearly the same result — gene selectivity wasn't the deciding factor, added coverage was, and it moved the wrong way. This lever is now a tested, honest null, not an untried one; endothelial identity moving with a threshold this arbitrary is still the same caveat GBM's single-marker PECAM1 gate carried.
- **The clustering correction is doing real, density-changing work here, which makes every MVD-dependent number linking-distance-sensitive in a way GBM's was not** (§2.2, §3.3). Any single-number headline (this report uses 20 µm, matching GBM's convention) should be read against the full 15-30 µm sweep, not in isolation.
- **No whole-slide, unbiased-sampling MVD anchor exists for breast IDC** (checked directly against Marien et al. 2016 full text, PMID 27583442, and confirmed absent). The best available quantitative density anchor (Weidner's hotspot method) is deliberately biased high by construction, and its conversion to vessels/mm² here borrows a field-area figure from a different paper's microscope (PMID 7539869), not one stated in the Weidner papers themselves. Any 'HIGH by Nx' verdict for breast MVD is therefore weaker evidence than the equivalent GBM verdict, which compared against a method-validated, whole-slide, unbiased anchor.
- **No directly-measured intercapillary (Voronoi) spacing study for breast IDC could be found**, unlike GBM's Yoshii & Sugiyama 1988 anchor (PMID 3359450). The vessel-vessel nearest-neighbour distances reported here (27-55 µm across the linking sweep) therefore have no external reference point at all, only an internal (CSR-null) one.
- **Poisson expectations are a model, not a measurement**, exactly as for GBM: they describe what a uniformly random vessel field at a given density implies, not a directly observed distance distribution.
- **2D, straight-line, single plane.** Same caveat as GBM: a 2D section overstates the true 3D distance to the nearest vessel (most nearby vessels are out of plane), which — if anything — makes the 'MVD too high relative to a biased-high hotspot anchor' finding conservative rather than overstated.
- **Thomlinson & Gray measured lung carcinoma**, reused here purely as the general O2-diffusion-physics reference, not a breast-specific or GBM-specific measurement, exactly as in the GBM report.
- **Nothing here validates the field's VALUES** — only the geometry of the endothelium behind them. `vascular_density` remains a relative, max-normalised quantity with no absolute perfused fraction.

## 6. What would fix this

1. ~~Use more of the 15 endothelial genes this panel actually measures, rather than 3.~~ **Tried, and it does not help.** A follow-up prototype found richer marker-based typing roughly halves the CSR tail ratio (moving toward randomness, not away from it) and pushes it below 1.0 at wider linking distances — see the caveat above and `results/sweep/endothelial_marker_richness_2026-07-29T19-07-43Z.md` for the full sweep. This was the one concrete lever breast had that GBM's single-PECAM1-gene panel never could try; it is now a tested null, not an open option.
2. **Find or run a whole-slide, unbiased-sampling breast MVD study** (the Marien-2016-style follow-up that paper's own Discussion calls for and that could not be located here) — without it, every absolute-density comparison in this report rests on a deliberately-biased-high hotspot anchor with an inferred field-area conversion.
3. **A between-case CD34-area-fraction or MVD range study for breast IDC**, analogous to GBM's Sipos et al. 2024 (PMID 38928515), would let §2.6's heterogeneity number be judged against a real target instead of a within-tumour compartment proxy.
4. **Resolve which vessel-density definition the CSR test should use as its headline** (§3.4) — raw endothelial cell or clustered vessel object — rather than reporting both because neither dominates. This is a genuine open methodological question this report surfaces, not one it resolves.
