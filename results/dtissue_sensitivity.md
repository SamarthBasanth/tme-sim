# D_tissue falsifiability sweep — do the breast 3-D:2-D verdicts survive a measured tissue diffusivity?

Generated by `experiments/dtissue_sensitivity.py` on the same REAL Xenium breast section as `results/kill_calibration_breast.md` (`data_cache/xenium_breast_janesick/rep1`; Janesick et al. 2023, PMID 38114474), at **`dx = 24 um`** (grid 230x315 — **NOT the published 12 um grid; see §7.2**), with the same `dt = 10 s`, the same 6 h window, the same seed 0, the same `PDConfig`, the same p99 bath-matching convention and the same frozen literature bands as the published report. 167,780 cells, 89,785 tumour. 120 transport solves, 62 min.

> **This is a falsifiability test, not a calibration.** No D_tissue value was selected on the basis of how many verdicts it makes pass. The output is the shape of the response curve across the whole literature-defensible range, plus an honest statement of which verdicts are robust and which are artefacts of one parameter choice. The recommendation at the end is justified by measurement provenance only.

> ### STALE TRANSPORT WARNING — this sweep's exposure fields predate the `_TUMOR_SIGMA` units fix
>
> The interstitial-pressure solve smooths the tumour-occupancy field with a gaussian whose width was, until commit `5d6e8e4`, expressed in GRID CELLS (`_TUMOR_SIGMA = 5.0`) and therefore tracked the mesh. It is now a physical length (`_TUMOR_SIGMA_UM = 60.0`). **`dx = 12 um` is the exact fixed point** (`60.0 / 12.0 == 5.0` in IEEE-754), so every shipped `dx = 12` result is bit-identical — but this sweep ran at **`dx = 24 um`**, where the old constant gave a 120 um smoothing instead of 60 um. `results/tumor_sigma_units.md` §3 measures the consequence on the mean tissue IFP at `dx = 24`: **0.181899 -> 0.132363**. The velocity field feeds the convection term of every transport solve behind the table below.
>
> **The exposure fields and hence every RATIO in this document were solved before that fix and have NOT been re-solved here** — this regeneration re-scored stored ratios against the corrected literature bands and solved no PDE (`results/fallback_rule_propagation.md`). The band decision is exact regardless, because it consumes only the stored scalar. The ratios are not: they would move by an unmeasured amount on a re-run, and the §7.3 confirmation set at the published `dx = 12 um` grid is the part of this file that is unaffected by construction. Re-solving costs 120 transport solves and was deliberately not done here.

The single most important structural fact, established in §2 and quantified in §5: `alpha` is fit so the model reproduces a MEASURED doxorubicin penetration depth, and that fit makes `alpha` nearly proportional to D. In the mode the codebase actually runs (`recal`), a several-fold change in D_tissue is therefore absorbed almost entirely by the re-fitted sink, and the scored 3-D:2-D ratio barely moves. The diffusivity being wrong is a real defect, but it is a defect in ATTRIBUTION (how much drug is claimed to be consumed perivascularly), not primarily in the published 3-D:2-D verdicts.

## 1. Where D_tissue actually enters the solver (traced, not assumed)

The transport PDE reads `DrugParams.diffusivity` (um^2/s) and applies the engine's own ECM tortuosity coupling `D_eff(x,y) = diffusivity * (1 - 0.6 * ecm_density(x,y))` (`tmesim/physics/diffusion.py`, `ALPHA_TORTUOSITY = 0.6`). `DrugParams.diffusivity` is not a constant in a table for these drugs — it is computed by the molecular compile:

| step | doxorubicin value | source |
|---|---|---|
| hydrodynamic radius | 351.31 um^2/s implies r = 0.539 nm | `stokes_einstein.hydrodynamic_radius_nm` (empirical MW scaling) |
| D_free (Stokes-Einstein) | **351.31 um^2/s = 3.513e-06 cm^2/s** | `stokes_einstein.free_diffusivity`, T = 310 K, eta = 1.2e-3 Pa.s |
| x Brownian-dynamics + Ogston-Renkin hindrance 0.5259 | 184.75 um^2/s | `compile_drug.build_profile` |
| x ECM tortuosity at the calibration reference ecm = 0.4 (factor 0.76) | **140.41 um^2/s = 1.404e-06 cm^2/s** | `physics/diffusion._effective_diffusivity` |
| **D_tissue / D_free** | **0.3997** | — |

So the 0.40 ratio the literature pass flagged is confirmed to 4 significant figures (**0.3997**) — but it is NOT a hard-coded tortuosity constant. It is the *product* of two independently-derived hindrance factors (0.5259 from the particle sim and mesh gate, 0.76 from the ECM coupling) that happen to land on the canonical inert-solute value. The criticism stands regardless: neither factor knows anything about cationic binding, DNA intercalation or endosomal sequestration, so the model has no mechanism by which doxorubicin could be hindered more than an inert solute of the same size.

**The sweep is applied as a single multiplicative factor `s = D_target / 1.404e-06` on every drug's `DrugParams.diffusivity`.** Because the ECM factor is multiplicative and untouched, this scales the whole spatial D field by exactly `s` and preserves the per-drug relative diffusivity ordering.

**Trap check (the brief's KNOWN TRAP).** `kill_calibration.unit_transport()` calls `simulate_drug_transport` directly, so no `PDEConfig` flag could have reached the solver. D reaches it only through the `DrugParams` object, which is what this script overrides via `dataclasses.replace`. Every exposure field in this sweep was fingerprinted and asserted bit-distinct from every other point of the same (mode, drug) series before any result below was computed; the assertion is in `run()` and it passed:

## 2. D_tissue is NOT an independent parameter — it is coupled to `alpha`

`tmesim.validation.calibration.calibrate_alpha` solves in closed form for the ONE perivascular-sink constant so that doxorubicin's analytic penetration length `L = sqrt(D_eff / k_eff)` equals the MEASURED anchor **64.92 um** (45 um half-distance / ln2; Primeau et al. 2005, **PMID 16361566**):

```
alpha = (D_eff / L*^2 - k_passive) / uptake_proxy
```

with `k_passive = 6.3080e-05 /s` and `uptake_proxy = 2.2375e-04 /s`, giving the shipping **alpha = 148.6082**. Because `k_passive << alpha*uptake_proxy`, alpha is very nearly PROPORTIONAL to D. Lowering D therefore lowers the fitted sink by nearly the same factor, and `L` barely moves. Reporting a sweep that ignores this would be reporting a scenario the codebase does not actually produce. Both are therefore run:

| mode | what it is | what happens to L |
|---|---|---|
| `frozen` | D changed by hand, published alpha kept | `L` moves as `sqrt(D)` |
| `recal` | D changed AND alpha re-solved against the SAME measured doxorubicin penetration anchor — **what the repo actually does, because `calibrate_alpha()` is called at run time from the compiled molecule** | `L` pinned by the measurement, nearly invariant |

## 5. The cancellation factor — how much of a delivery change reaches the score

The scored quantity is a 3-D:2-D kill RATIO computed within one drug on one tissue, with the plasma level re-solved at every point so that the p99 best-perfused tumour cell sits exactly at the bath concentration (`kill_calibration.decompose`). **A change to D that scaled the exposure field uniformly would cancel EXACTLY** — algebraically, if `e -> s*e` then `ref_unit -> s*ref_unit`, `plasma = c_bath/(s*ref_unit)`, and `E = plasma * s * e` is unchanged. The ratio can therefore only respond to a change in the SHAPE of the exposure distribution, never to its scale. The numbers below measure how much of the change is shape and how much is scale.

Two absolute quantities are tracked against the scored ratio: the mean tumour exposure per uM of plasma (raw delivery) and the model's kill fraction at the repo-nominal dose (the product's actual absolute output). `fraction surviving = ln(ratio fold) / ln(absolute fold)`: 0.0 = total cancellation, 1.0 = no cancellation, **above 1.0 = the score is MORE sensitive than the absolute output, i.e. anti-cancellation**. `cancellation fold` compares the SIZES of the two changes direction-free (`exp|ln fold|` of each), so a value of 3x means the absolute quantity moved three times as far as the score.

## 6. The D_free axis — and the sim's nominal temperature

**The sim DOES have a temperature concept, and it is already 37 C.** `tmesim.molecular.compile_drug.build_profile` takes `temperature_K = 310.0` (documented as body temperature) and passes it to `stokes_einstein.free_diffusivity(radius, temperature_K=310.0, viscosity_pa_s=1.2e-3)`. So the literature pass's inference that our D_free matches Stokes-Einstein *at 30 C* is **not** what the code does — the code is at 310 K and gets 3.513e-06 cm^2/s from an elevated INTERSTITIAL-FLUID viscosity of 1.2e-3 Pa.s (about 1.7x water at 37 C, ~6.9e-4 Pa.s, which the repo's own `permeability.py` uses for the membrane calculation). The low D_free is a viscosity assumption, not a temperature error. That distinction matters, because 1.2e-3 Pa.s is a *defensible* choice for interstitial fluid whereas 30 C would simply be a bug.

**D_free is not an independent degree of freedom in this solver.** The engine never sees D_free; it sees `DrugParams.diffusivity = D_free x hindrance`. Multiplying D_free by a factor at fixed hindrance is therefore the SAME RUN as multiplying D_tissue by that factor — it is already covered by this sweep, and needs no extra solves. Moving D_free to the suggested 4.27e-6 cm^2/s is a **1.215x** increase, which at fixed hindrance would put D_tissue at **1.707e-06 cm^2/s** — i.e. it moves D_tissue in the WRONG direction, further ABOVE every direct tumour-tissue measurement, and lands just past the top of the swept range. Read off the monotone trend at the top of the tables in section 3, that change makes the 3-D penalty smaller still.

The combined statement is the one worth acting on: if D_free is corrected upward to 4.27e-6 cm^2/s AND the hindrance is set from the MEASURED tumour-tissue D_tissue/D_free ratios for real drugs (0.06-0.13 for doxorubicin, 0.045-0.083 for tirapazamine in multicellular layers) rather than from the inert-solute tortuosity rule, D_tissue lands at **2.56e-07 - 5.55e-07 cm^2/s** — the bottom third of this sweep, and consistent with the three direct measurements. Correcting D_free WITHOUT correcting the hindrance would make the model worse.

## 8. Recommendation — justified by measurement provenance, NOT by score

The rule this section obeys: the recommended value is the one with the best measurement provenance, chosen BEFORE looking at how many verdicts it produces. For the record, the PASS count at the recommended value is stated afterwards, not used to reach it.

**Provenance of the candidates.**

* The shipping **1.404e-06 cm^2/s** has no direct measurement behind it at all. Its D_free leg is first-principles Stokes-Einstein (defensible) and its hindrance leg is a particle sim plus an Ogston-Renkin fibre-matrix gate — both of which model EXCLUDED VOLUME only. Neither has any term for the cationic binding, DNA intercalation or endosomal sequestration that define doxorubicin's transport. Landing on the canonical inert-solute ratio 0.40 is exactly what an excluded-volume-only model should do, and exactly why it is the wrong number for this drug. Sykova & Nicholson (Physiol Rev 2008, **PMID 18923183**) state the restriction themselves.
* Three DIRECT measurements exist in real, cellularised tissue, and they agree with each other far better than any of them agrees with the shipping value:
    * **2.9e-07 cm^2/s** — Toley 2013 LS174T 3-D cell mass (PMID 23860772) (4.8x below the shipping value)
    * **5.0e-07 cm^2/s** — Weinberg 2007 rabbit VX2 liver carcinoma in vivo (PMID 17900740) (2.8x below the shipping value)
    * **6.7e-07 cm^2/s** — Qian 2003 rat liver in vivo (PMID 12932647) (2.1x below the shipping value)
* Their geometric mean is **4.60e-07 cm^2/s**, spanning 2.9e-07-6.7e-07. Independently, measured D_tissue/D_free ratios in cellularised tumour tissue are 0.06-0.13 for doxorubicin and 0.045-0.083 for tirapazamine in multicellular layers — 3-8x below the 0.40 this model uses, and consistent with the absolute numbers.

**Recommendation: set D_tissue = 4.6e-07 cm^2/s** (the geometric mean of the three direct measurements), and carry **2.9e-07-6.7e-07 cm^2/s** as the reported uncertainty band rather than a point value. That is a **3.1x reduction** from the shipping value. Every number in that recommendation traces to Toley 2013 / Weinberg 2007 / Qian 2003; none of it traces to this sweep's PASS counts.

**Two conditions attach to it, and both are load-bearing:**

1. **`alpha` MUST be re-calibrated with it (mode `recal`), not left frozen.** The measured doxorubicin penetration depth (Primeau 2005, PMID 16361566) is an independent measurement this model already reproduces by construction: the published 40-50 um half-distance is an e-folding band of **57.7-72.1 um**. Changing D without re-solving alpha silently breaks that anchor — at the recommended D, mode `frozen` gives **L = 37.0 um**, well below the measured band, whereas mode `recal` holds it at 64.9 um by construction. A model that gets the diffusivity right and the penetration depth wrong is not an improvement.
2. **Correcting D_free alone would make the model worse, not better.** Section 6: raising D_free to 4.27e-6 cm^2/s at fixed hindrance raises D_tissue further ABOVE the measurements. D_free and the hindrance have to move together, and the measured 0.06-0.13 ratio is the thing that should replace the 0.40.

**The deeper structural point, which the sweep makes unavoidable.** Because alpha is fit so that `L = sqrt(D_eff/k_eff)` matches a measured penetration depth, the measurement constrains only the RATIO `D/k`. D_tissue and the perivascular sink strength are not separately identified by anything this model is currently anchored to. Lowering D_tissue 3x and lowering the inferred sink 3x leaves the gradient the model actually predicts unchanged — which is why the scored ratios move so little in mode `recal`. Getting D_tissue right therefore does not by itself buy a better prediction; it buys a correctly-attributed one, and it changes the claim the model makes about how much drug is being consumed perivascularly by roughly the same factor. That claim is currently unmeasured and should be flagged as such.

For the record, and stated only after the fact: at the swept point nearest the recommended 4.6e-07 cm^2/s, the formal verdicts are

* mode `frozen`, on the PUBLISHED dx = 12 um grid (D = 4.559e-07 cm^2/s): **4/4 PASS** (of 6 swept drugs; 2 are NOT TESTABLE and are counted in no denominator) — 5-fluorouracil NOT TESTABLE, cisplatin PASS, docetaxel PASS, doxorubicin PASS, epirubicin PASS, paclitaxel NOT TESTABLE
* mode `recal`, on the PUBLISHED dx = 12 um grid (D = 4.559e-07 cm^2/s): **3/4 PASS** (of 6 swept drugs; 2 are NOT TESTABLE and are counted in no denominator) — 5-fluorouracil NOT TESTABLE, cisplatin PASS, docetaxel FAIL, doxorubicin PASS, epirubicin PASS, paclitaxel NOT TESTABLE

Note the direction of that result, because it is the opposite of what a rigged sweep would produce: in `recal` — the mode the codebase actually runs, and the mode the recommendation requires — the measurement-backed diffusivity does **not** rescue the one published FAIL. Docetaxel still fails. The recommendation is made anyway, on provenance.

## 9. Limitations of THIS sweep

1. **A single global scale factor is applied to all 6 drugs.** The three direct measurements are doxorubicin (and one small-molecule-in-liver) specific. The defect they expose — an excluded-volume-only hindrance model applied to interacting solutes — is structural and applies to every drug in the panel, but the SIZE of the correction is almost certainly drug-specific (cisplatin is small, neutral-ish and weakly bound; docetaxel is large and highly protein-bound). A per-drug binding-aware hindrance model is the real fix; this sweep is the first-order propagation, not that fix.
2. **Only the 6 formally-testable drugs were swept.** The other 4 panel drugs (cyclophosphamide, tamoxifen, vinorelbine, palbociclib) have no literature band at all, so no verdict of theirs could flip. Their ratios will move exactly as these do.
3. **Only the 1x IC50 reporting concentration was swept.** The published report evaluates the decomposition at 0.1/0.3/1/3/10x IC50 and notes that F_penetration climbs toward 1 as the Hill term saturates. The verdict is taken at 1x IC50, so that is what is swept, but the D-sensitivity at other concentrations is not characterised here.
4. **The p99 bath-matching convention is held fixed.** The GBM report found the cisplatin verdict to be convention-dependent (pass at p99, fail at max-exposure). This sweep does not cross that with the D axis, so a joint convention-x-diffusivity sensitivity is still unmeasured.
5. **Everything here inherits the delivery-side error already logged in `results/breast_kp_validation.md`** (a blood-brain-barrier permeability applied to breast tissue, and a one-way delivery source with no back-flux). This sweep varies D with those two defects still present; fixing them could change the D-response, and the interaction has not been tested.
6. **The 3 direct-measurement anchors are reported as the literature pass supplied them.** They were not independently re-verified against the primary papers in this run; the PMIDs are carried through unchanged so they can be.
7. **An unplanned finding that is arguably more urgent than the one this sweep was commissioned for: docetaxel changes verdict between `dx = 12 um` and `dx = 24 um` (§7.2), on a grid the repo documents as converged.** The published docetaxel FAIL is the only FAIL in the panel and it does not survive a 2x grid coarsening, which is a smaller perturbation than the 3.1x diffusivity correction it DOES survive (§7.3, `recal`). A grid-convergence re-check of the breast panel is a separate piece of work this sweep did not do and cannot substitute for; nothing here should be read as having tested it.
