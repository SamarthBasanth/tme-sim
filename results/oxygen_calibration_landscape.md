# Oxygen calibration landscape: is there a real target for `OxygenConfig.consumption`?

**Status: research only. No code changed.** This is a narrow, targeted follow-up to `results/landscape_imaging_derived.md` (which surveyed hypoxia PET/OE-MRI broadly and is not repeated here). The question here is specific: does an absolute, MEASURED oxygen value exist in real glioma tissue — pO2 in mmHg, or an oxygen consumption rate (OCR) in comparable diffusion-model units — that could calibrate `OxygenConfig.consumption` (`tmesim/physics/oxygen.py`, wired from `tmesim/core/config.py:76-82`) and/or sanity-check the `PDConfig.hypoxia_resistance` / `quiescent_resistance` kill multipliers (`tmesim/core/config.py:86-91`, used at `tmesim/cells/pkpd.py:230` and `tmesim/validation/kill_calibration.py:454-455`)?

## Bottom line, stated plainly

**Yes for pO2 thresholds and hypoxic fractions — real, glioma-specific, human, multi-study data exists and is stronger than what this project currently uses (nothing).** **No for a directly usable, glioma-specific tissue-level oxygen *consumption rate* in units comparable to `OxygenConfig.consumption`** — the closest thing found is a non-glioma theoretical modeling paper's illustrative OCR range, and glioma-specific OCR data that does exist (Seahorse assays) is cell-line, monolayer data that needs at least two additional, nontrivial conversions before it says anything about tissue. And **the unit conversion from any of this into `OxygenConfig.consumption` is nontrivial for a reason specific to this codebase, not just generically**: tme-sim's oxygen field `O` is a dimensionless relative saturation in `[0,1]` (see `docs/MATHEMATICAL_MODEL.md:748`, `tmesim/physics/oxygen.py:24-25`), not a pO2 in mmHg, and the model has never defined what `O = 1` corresponds to in mmHg. That missing mapping — not the literature search — is the binding constraint on using any of the numbers below directly. This is explained in detail in the final section, which outlines rather than performs the conversion, per the scope of this task.

The kill multipliers (`hypoxia_resistance = 0.35`, `quiescent_resistance = 0.5`) remain **entirely unaddressed by anything found in this pass**: no source below gives a directly comparable dose-response or resistance-ratio number for glioma cells specifically at defined pO2 bands. That gap is reported honestly at the end rather than papered over.

## 1. Glioma/GBM tissue pO2 — real, direct, polarographic-electrode measurements (usable for the hypoxia *threshold*, not the consumption rate)

These are the classic Eppendorf/Clark-type polarographic needle-electrode measurements the task asked about specifically. Four independent human studies were found, all intraoperative, all glioma- or GBM-specific:

| Study | n (patients) | Tissue | Technique | Key reported pO2 |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Rampling et al. 1994 | 20 | malignant brain tumours (HGG + metastatic) | polarographic O2 electrode, 6 tracks/patient, 192 total readings, 22 mm path length | "significant regions of hypoxia" in both intrinsic and metastatic tumours (qualitative in the abstract; exact median **UNVERIFIED** — full text not accessed in this pass) |
| Collingridge, Piepmeier, Rockwell, Knisely 1999 | not stated in abstract | human glioma **and** surrounding peritumoural brain | Eppendorf-style polarographic electrode | **Both glioma and peritumoural brain contain regions with pO2 < 2.5 mmHg** |
| Lally, Rockwell, Fischer, Collingridge, Piepmeier, Knisely 2006 | 23 (13 HGG, 10 LGG) | glioma, stratified by grade | Eppendorf pO2 Histograph, intraoperative | **Median tumour pO2, whole cohort = 5.1 mmHg.** Cohort re-dichotomized at 2.0, 5.1, and 10.0 mmHg; grade was prognostic for survival (p = 0.0012), median pO2 itself was **not** an independent prognostic factor in this small, heterogeneous series |
| Beppu, Kamada, Yoshida et al. 2002 | 18 (newly diagnosed GBM) | **GBM specifically**, intratumoral vs. peritumoral | Disposable Clark-type electrode via CT-guided stereotactic placement, awake/asleep/O2-inhalation/diuretic/HBO conditions | Intratumoral pO2 ≈ **15.3 ± 2.3 mmHg** (awake); peritumoral ≈ **59.8 ± 6.5 mmHg**; arterial reference ≈ **109.2 mmHg**; both fell further asleep; 100% O2 inhalation did not raise either; osmotic diuretics raised peritumoral only; HBO raised both. **An apparent companion source, flagged UNVERIFIED.** A search snippet attributed to an *Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys* 2004 supplement item (same title pattern/author group as Lally 2006, likely an ASTRO meeting-abstract precursor of the same Yale cohort) reports **median pO2 = 14.9 mmHg (range 0.1–40.1) for LGG vs. 2.9 mmHg (range 0.4–30.2) for HGG** — a grade-stratified breakdown of the same 5.1 mmHg whole-cohort figure above. The page (`redjournal.org/article/S0360-3016(04)01190-3`) returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch in this pass, so this number is reported here **UNVERIFIED against the primary source** — it is plausible and internally consistent with Lally 2006, but was not independently confirmed.

**Interlaboratory variability caveat — directly relevant to how tightly any single number above should be trusted.** Nozue et al. 1997 ran the *same* polarographic technique on the same murine tumour model (FSaII fibrosarcoma, not glioma) across six laboratories and found the median tumour pO2 varied **1.5–5.6 mmHg** across labs, and normal-tissue median varied **28–38 mmHg**, with the authors concluding explicitly that "caution needs to be exercised in using the absolute, median, or distribution of pO2 values" across labs/methods. Nozue M, Lee I, Yuan F, Teicher BA, Brizel DM, Dewhirst MW, et al. *J Surg Oncol* 1997;66(1):30-38. PMID [9290690](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9290690/), DOI [10.1002/(SICI)1096-9098(199709)66:1<30::AID-JSO7>3.0.CO;2-O](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1096-9098(199709)66:1%3C30::AID-JSO7%3E3.0.CO;2-O). This does not invalidate the glioma numbers above (the 1.5–5.6 mmHg tumour spread it reports is, coincidentally, close to the 2.9–5.1 mmHg glioma range above), but it means a single point value should not be treated as a precise ground truth — a plausible **range** (roughly 2–15 mmHg intratumoral, grade- and technique-dependent) is the honest calibration target, not a single number.

**Normal (non-tumour) brain pO2, for contrast — UNVERIFIED precision, general physiology literature, not independently fetched from primary sources in this pass:** commonly cited figures put white-matter pO2 around 20–40 mmHg and average brain tissue pO2 around 10 mmHg, with cortical measurements (proximity-to-arteriole dependent) spanning roughly 5–100 mmHg and averaging ~23 mmHg in one older series. Note this is in tension with Beppu's own **peritumoral** value of ~60 mmHg above — "peritumoral" brain adjacent to a GBM is not the same tissue state as normal distant cortex (mass effect, edema, altered perfusion), so the two shouldn't be equated. Flagged **UNVERIFIED** pending a primary-source check.

**What this section is usable for:** `OxygenConfig.hypoxia_threshold` currently sets the HYPOXIC/QUIESCENT/VIABLE bands purely on the model's own dimensionless `O` (`< 0.2` / `0.2–0.4` / `≥ 0.4`, `docs/MATHEMATICAL_MODEL.md:855-867`) with **no absolute anchor at all**. The numbers above give a real, glioma-specific, multiply-replicated absolute floor: intratumoral GBM pO2 clusters in the **single digits to teens of mmHg**, with a literature-standard "hypoxic" cutoff around **2.5 mmHg** (used definitionally in Collingridge 1999 and the OER-collapse point referenced by Grimes et al. 2014, §2 below) and a broader **10 mmHg** cutoff also in common use (Lally 2006's dichotomization, and the pimonidazole threshold in §3). **This is a real threshold target for the model's absolute level, even without solving the mmHg-to-`O` conversion** — see §4 for what using it would actually require.

## 2. Oxygen consumption rate (OCR) in comparable diffusion-model units — the harder ask

This is where the search came up **short of a glioma-specific number**, and that gap should be stated plainly rather than papered over with a near-miss.

**What was found: a general (non-glioma) reaction-diffusion oxygen model with an explicit, absolute-unit consumption constant.** Grimes DR, Fletcher AG, Partridge M. "Oxygen consumption dynamics in steady-state tumour models." *R Soc Open Sci* 2014;1(1):140080. PMID [26064525](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26064525/), PMC [4448765](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4448765/), DOI [10.1098/rsos.140080](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.140080). This paper solves essentially the same class of steady-state reaction-diffusion equation tme-sim solves (§4.1 of `docs/MATHEMATICAL_MODEL.md`), for spherical (multicellular tumour spheroid) and cylindrical (Krogh-type vessel) geometries:

$$D\,\nabla^2 p = \Omega \cdot a(p)$$

where `p` is oxygen partial pressure **in mmHg** (not a dimensionless saturation), `Ω = 3.0318×10⁷ mmHg·kg/m³` is a Henry's-law conversion constant, and `a(p)` is the oxygen consumption rate in SI units of **volume O2 per unit tissue mass per unit time (m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻¹)**. The paper simulates across an illustrative "typical literature" range of maximal consumption rate:

- **`a_max` = 2.5×10⁻⁷ to 1.5×10⁻⁶ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻¹**, equivalently **`Ω·a_max` = 7.50–45.45 mmHg O2 s⁻¹** — i.e., the rate at which a unit of fully-consuming tissue would draw down local oxygen partial pressure, in mmHg per second. This is the single most directly comparable unit form to what `OxygenConfig.consumption` would need to be if the model were re-expressed in absolute pO2.
- The diffusion coefficient used ("close to that of water") is consistent with tme-sim's own `OxygenConfig.diffusivity = 2000 µm²/s` (typical tissue O2 diffusivity is reported around 1.5–2×10⁻⁵ cm² s⁻¹ ≈ 1500–2000 µm²/s) — a useful independent cross-check that tme-sim's *diffusivity* constant is in the right ballpark, even though diffusivity was not the object of this search.
- **Important structural finding, not just a units gap.** The literature default in this space (and the one Grimes' paper is built around) treats OCR as **zeroth-order** — a constant `a_max`, independent of local oxygen tension — transitioning to Michaelis–Menten (hyperbolic) kinetics only very close to anoxia, with a half-saturation constant `Km ≲ 1 mmHg` reported across the literature Grimes surveys. **tme-sim's sink term is `−c·ρ_tum·O`: proportional to `O` everywhere**, i.e. first-order in oxygen across the *entire* range [0,1], not just near-zero. That is the opposite qualitative behavior from what this literature treats as standard: a first-order-everywhere sink automatically throttles down consumption as tissue oxygenates, which will systematically under-predict hypoxia relative to a model where cells keep consuming at close to their maximal rate until oxygen is nearly gone. **This means recalibrating the numeric value of `consumption` alone, without addressing this functional-form mismatch, is unlikely to reproduce a realistic pO2 profile even if the constant is "right" in some averaged sense** — this is a modeling-choice finding, not merely a missing-number finding, and is worth flagging to whoever eventually does this work.
- Grimes' paper is explicitly **not glioma-specific** — it is a general theoretical/methods paper (MCTS + generic Krogh-cylinder geometry), and its consumption-rate range is described as "typical of the literature" without a single traceable primary glioma measurement behind it in the text extracted. Treat it as a **plausibility range for tumour tissue in general**, not a glioma number.

**What was found but is NOT directly usable without further conversion: glioma cell-line OCR from Seahorse assays.** Two papers measure oxygen consumption rate (OCR, in the Seahorse-standard pmol O2/min, typically normalized per cell or per µg protein) in glioblastoma cell lines:
- Atif F, Yousuf S, Espinosa-Garcia C, Stein DG. "Progesterone Modulates Mitochondrial Functions in Human Glioblastoma Cells." *Mol Neurobiol* 2021;58(8):3805-3816. PMID [33847913](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33847913/), DOI [10.1007/s12035-021-02382-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12035-021-02382-1). Three human GBM cell lines, Seahorse mitochondrial stress test, basal/maximal respiration, ATP-linked and proton-leak OCR reported — **exact numeric OCR values UNVERIFIED in this pass** (not extracted from abstract; would require full-text access).
- Pike Winer LS, Wu M. "Rapid analysis of glycolytic and oxidative substrate flux of cancer cells in a microplate." *PLoS One* 2014;9(10):e109916. PMID [25360519](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25360519/), PMC [4215881](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4215881/), DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0109916](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0109916). Two GBM-derived lines (SF188 slow- and fast-proliferating variants), Seahorse XF, substantial baseline OCR reported qualitatively; **exact numeric values UNVERIFIED in this pass**.

Even with exact numbers in hand, Seahorse OCR is a **2D-monolayer, per-cell (or per-protein) rate measured in isolated culture**, not a 3D-tissue volumetric consumption rate. Converting it to something comparable to `consumption * rho_tum` would require an *additional*, separate assumption about in-tissue cell packing density (cells per unit tissue volume) on top of the mmHg/saturation conversion in §4 — and cultured monolayer metabolism is well known to differ from the metabolic state of cells in a hypoxic 3D tumour (Warburg shift, oxygen and nutrient gradients absent in a dish), so even a clean conversion would carry real uncertainty about whether it represents in vivo tissue behavior. This route is plausible in principle but is the weakest of those surveyed here.

**No glioma-tissue-level diffusion-model-derived consumption constant (i.e., a paper that measures pO2 gradients in glioma tissue directly and back-calculates a volumetric consumption rate the way Krogh-cylinder or spheroid-fitting papers do for other tumour types) was found in this search pass.** This is the single most valuable thing the task asked for, and it came up empty for glioma specifically — worth stating as a real (if disappointing) finding rather than stretching the Grimes or Seahorse numbers to fill the gap.

## 3. Hypoxia-marker (pimonidazole / EF5) calibration curves — an indirect route, and why it doesn't apply to this project's *existing* data

The task asked whether pimonidazole/EF5 IHC calibration curves exist that could translate a measured hypoxia-marker intensity into an approximate pO2. They do, in a general (non-glioma-specific-calibration) sense:

- **Pimonidazole**: binding is essentially zero above ~10 mmHg and increases sharply below that threshold — functionally closer to a step function around **10 mmHg** than a continuous calibration curve. Cited via Grimes et al. 2014 (§2 above) and corroborated by the broader literature on antibody-concentration-dependent pimonidazole/EF5 binding: Evans SM, Jenkins WT, Joiner B, Lord EM, Koch CJ. "Hypoxic heterogeneity in human tumors" work and follow-on methods papers, e.g. the antibody-concentration study: PMID [18494550](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18494550/), *Radiat Res* 2008;169(6):677-688 — reports binding for pimonidazole is independent of cell line, "zero above 10 mmHg, with an easily discriminated increase at lower pO2," but pO2-dependence of binding varies with antibody concentration used, which matters for treating any single staining protocol as absolutely calibrated.
- **EF5**: binds continuously across roughly **0.8–10 mmHg** (cell-line/binding-affinity dependent), and — unlike pimonidazole — has been used to generate genuinely quantitative per-pixel pO2 calibration curves in some published work (per Grimes et al. 2014's discussion). A **GBM-specific** published EF5→pO2 calibration curve was not found in this search pass — **UNVERIFIED for glioma specifically** — though EF5 has been used as a qualitative hypoxia marker in glioma models (e.g., Marotta D et al., "In vivo profiling of hypoxic gene expression in gliomas using the hypoxia marker EF5 and laser-capture microdissection," *Cancer Res* 2011;71(3):779-789, PMID [21266355](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21266355/), PMC [3071295](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3071295/), DOI [10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-10-3061](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-10-3061) — a 9L rat glioma model, EF5 used as a binary viable-hypoxic-vs-normoxic gate for laser-capture microdissection, not as a quantitative pO2 calibration).

**Why this route doesn't help tme-sim's current data, even in principle.** `tmesim/physics/oxygen_proxy.py` and `tmesim/reconstruct/cell_state.py` already document this exact distinction carefully: the project's own hypoxia proxy is built from **transcript counts** (VEGFA, CA9, ADM, NDRG1, SLC2A1 on Xenium/GEO panels — `tmesim/reconstruct/cell_state.py:100-116`, `tmesim/physics/oxygen_proxy.py:154-169`), which the module's own docstring is explicit are "hypoxia-**RESPONSE** transcripts" and "NOT an oxygen tension" (`tmesim/physics/oxygen_proxy.py:52`, `148-149`). Pimonidazole/EF5 calibration curves relate **protein-level IHC staining intensity** to pO2 — a different assay entirely (immunohistochemistry on a physically stained slide, not RNA transcript counts from spatial sequencing). tme-sim holds no IHC data of this type on any of its three real sections. This calibration route would only become usable if a **new wet-lab measurement** (pimonidazole or EF5 staining, ideally on tissue with matched Xenium serial sections) were performed — it cannot be applied retroactively to the transcript data already in the repository.

## 4. What would actually need to change in `tmesim/physics/oxygen.py` to use any of this — outlined, not performed

This section deliberately stops short of doing the conversion, per the scope of this task (research only, no code touched), but the mechanics are laid out so whoever picks this up next doesn't have to re-derive them.

**The core obstacle is not unit conversion arithmetic — it's a missing model decision.** `solve_oxygen` (`tmesim/physics/oxygen.py:181-239`) solves `D·∇²O + s·ρ_vasc·(1−O) − c·ρ_tum·O = 0` for `O ∈ [0,1]`, a **relative saturation**, not a pressure. The `(1−O)` source term is specifically written that way so supply switches off as `O→1` and the solution is bounded (`tmesim/physics/oxygen.py:20-22`, `docs/MATHEMATICAL_MODEL.md:755-757`). Nothing in the model currently states what physical pO2 corresponds to `O = 1` — there is no `p_ref` (e.g., "1 corresponds to 40 mmHg" or "to arterial ~100 mmHg") anywhere in `OxygenConfig`. **Before any mmHg number from §1 or §2 can be plugged in, someone has to decide this mapping** — it is a new modeling choice, not a unit lookup, and different reasonable choices (e.g., anchoring `O=1` to Beppu's peritumoral ~60 mmHg vs. to systemic arterial ~100 mmHg vs. to a generic "well-oxygenated tissue" convention of ~40 mmHg) would each imply a *different* numeric `consumption` even holding everything else fixed.

Once that reference is fixed, the outline of the conversion work is:

1. **Re-derive or re-interpret the governing equation in absolute units.** Either (a) keep `O` dimensionless but define `p = p_ref · O` and require that the *hypoxia threshold in mmHg* (§1: ~2.5–10 mmHg) map onto `hypoxia_threshold · p_ref`, which pins down `p_ref` given a chosen threshold-band interpretation; or (b) rewrite the PDE directly in mmHg (closer to Grimes' form in §2), which is the cleaner long-run fix but touches more of the module (the source term's `(1−O)` boundedness trick would need restating as `(p_ref − p)`, or an explicit Dirichlet/consumption-saturation model).
2. **Resolve the zeroth-order vs. first-order sink mismatch (§2).** tme-sim's `−c·ρ_tum·O` is first-order in oxygen everywhere; the literature default is zeroth-order (constant, `Vmax`) down to `Km ≲ 1 mmHg`. If the intent is to keep the current functional form, that should be a stated, deliberate choice, not an accident inherited from wanting a bounded linear system — the current form was very likely chosen for numerical convenience (the `A = -D·L + diag(reaction)` system needs `reaction ≥ 0` and linear-in-`O` to stay a single sparse linear solve, `tmesim/physics/oxygen.py:229-236`); moving to a Michaelis–Menten or zeroth-order sink would make the system nonlinear and require iteration, which is a real implementation cost, not just a parameter change.
3. **Convert `ρ_tum`'s units.** `_tumor_density` (`tmesim/physics/oxygen.py:106-137`) expresses tumour density as cells per `TUMOR_REFERENCE_AREA_UM2 = 144 µm²` **2D tile** (a section-plane areal density), whereas Grimes' `a(p)` in §2 is per **kg of 3D tissue mass**. Bridging these requires an assumed tissue thickness and cell density/packing assumption (e.g., converting a 2D Xenium section's areal density to an assumed-3D volumetric density) — this is exactly the kind of 2D-section-vs-3D-volume gap `results/landscape_imaging_derived.md` §3.3 already flags as a recurring, non-trivial problem for this codebase, now showing up again for oxygen specifically.
4. **Only then** does a literature OCR number (§2, if a glioma-specific one is ever found, or the Grimes general range as a rough plausibility check) translate into a candidate value for `consumption`, and even then it inherits every UNVERIFIED flag attached to its source above.

**Recommended immediate, lower-effort step, if any is wanted before the full conversion:** use the §1 pO2 thresholds (2.5 mmHg severe-hypoxia, ~10 mmHg the broader "hypoxic" cutoff used in Lally 2006 and pimonidazole) purely as a **plausibility check on the predicted hypoxic *fraction***, without touching the PDE's units at all — i.e., ask "does a 0.0% hypoxic fraction, or a 94% hypoxic fraction, look more or less plausible than a hypoxic fraction in the range these four human-glioma studies would imply for a typical GBM section?" rather than trying to match a specific mmHg value. That is a much weaker (ordinal, not quantitative) use of this literature, but it requires none of the unit-conversion machinery above and could be stated in `results/heldout_patient.md` / `results/real_drug_panel.md` today as an honest bound: the four studies in §1 make a **0.0% hypoxic fraction on a GBM section implausible** (all four studies found *substantial* sub-2.5-to-10-mmHg regions in every glioma tumour measured, none reported hypoxia-free tumours), which corroborates — with real external evidence, not just internal reasoning — the project's own existing conclusion that 0.0% "is not credible."

## 5. What was NOT found — the kill-multiplier gap remains completely open

Nothing in this search pass addresses `PDConfig.hypoxia_resistance = 0.35` or `quiescent_resistance = 0.5` (`tmesim/core/config.py:89-90`) at all. These would need a dose-response or resistance-ratio measurement (e.g., surviving fraction of glioma cells at defined pO2 bands under drug or radiation exposure, ideally TMZ-relevant given this project's flagship drug) — a different literature search than the one performed here (this pass targeted oxygen *tension and consumption*, not *resistance magnitude*). `tmesim/validation/kill_calibration.py:1953,1997` already flags these multipliers as "unvalidated priors" internally; nothing here changes that status. A dedicated follow-up search for glioma/GBM radio- or chemo-resistance ratios as a function of measured or categorical hypoxia (e.g., studies stratifying TMZ or radiation response by pimonidazole/EF5-positive vs. -negative regions) would be the natural next piece of this work, and was out of scope for this pass.
