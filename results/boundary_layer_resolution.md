# Is there a length scale the shipped dx = 12 um grid cannot resolve, and where?

Generated by `experiments/boundary_layer_resolution.py` (stages 0-4). Raw records:
`results/sweep/blayer_trap_check.json`, `blayer_lengthscales.json`,
`blayer_source_vs_dx.json`, `blayer_controlled.json`, `blayer_cost.json`,
`blayer_analysis.json`.

**The hypothesis under test**, from `results/transvascular_exchange_revalidation.md`
section 5: the gradient-driven delivery law introduces a perivascular boundary
layer `delta = sqrt(D_eff/(PS + clearance + binding))` that "collapses to roughly
15-17 um for `bbb_penetrance` >~ 0.6", against a shipped `dx = 12 um`.

That same section is annotated as **REFUTED on its directional consequence**: it
predicted from this argument that finer grids would push exchange-ON values DOWN;
that was measured and the values went UP by 1.3-2.6 %. So this report measures the
direction rather than reasoning to it, and it tests the mechanism on a problem
with a closed-form answer rather than only on tissue.

---

## 0. Bottom line

1. **The 15-17 um figure is CORRECT.** Recomputed from the actual compiled
   `DrugParams` and the actual reconstructed fields, `delta` is **14.5-16.7 um**
   for every drug with `bbb_penetrance` > 0.57, in both panels. At `dx = 12 um`
   that is **1.21-1.39 cells per boundary layer**. The premise of the concern is
   confirmed.

2. **The consequence does not follow.** On a controlled slab with an exact steady
   solution, one cell per layer costs only **-0.9 % in mean tissue concentration**
   at the real minimum `delta`. Fitted over 54 points, the error law is
   **`|rel err of mean| = 1.427e-3 * dx^1.939 * delta^-1.145`** (R^2 = 0.995) --
   clean second order in `dx`. This scheme needs about **1.1 cells per layer for
   1 %**, 1.6 for 0.5 %, 3.6 for 0.1 %. That is unusually forgiving, because the
   reaction term enters the implicit operator diagonally.

3. **The direction is DOWN, not up.** Under-resolving the layer **UNDER-estimates**
   tissue concentration (error is negative at every aligned point). The original
   `transvascular_exchange_revalidation.md` §5 prediction had the sign backwards, and this controlled measurement
   reproduces the sign of the refutation that was measured on tissue: refining
   12 -> 8 um raises the value.

4. **`delta` does NOT explain the docetaxel breast verdict flip, and cannot.**
   The shipped breast and GBM panels both run through
   `kill_calibration.unit_transport`, which is DECLARED `INERT` in
   `tmesim/validation/propagation.py` -- it passes no `exchange_k`. Those
   verdicts are **exchange-OFF numbers, so `delta` is not a term in the equation
   they solve.** Measured against the repo's own dx data, `delta` ranks the
   movers with the WRONG SIGN on that panel (rho = +0.47), while the exchange-OFF
   length `L = sqrt(D_eff/(clearance+binding))` ranks them at **rho = -0.94**.
   Docetaxel has the **shortest `L` in the panel (30.1 um)**.

5. **The binding scale is not the boundary layer -- it is the p99 normaliser.**
   With exchange OFF the domain-MEAN concentration has **exactly zero** grid
   error (a discrete conservation identity; measured max |rel err| = 2.1e-11 over all 180 points, i.e. the sparse solver's own tolerance). But the scored
   3-D:2-D ratio is not a mean: `kill_calibration.decompose` divides the whole
   exposure field by its **99th percentile**. On the controlled problem that
   shape statistic is off by **+8.3 % at dx = 12 um and +17.8 % at dx = 24 um**,
   and its error is **identical for exchange ON and OFF to 3 decimal places** --
   i.e. it is a source-geometry and peak-sampling error, not a boundary-layer
   error.

6. **Recommendation (dx, dt) pair.** For the exchange-ON delivery law and
   MEAN/AUC-type quantities, **`dx = 12 um, dt = 10 s` is adequate** (~1 % from
   the boundary layer). For any **p99-normalised or distribution-shaped**
   quantity -- which includes every 3-D:2-D verdict in this repo -- `dx = 12 um`
   carries a **+8.3 % bias**, and refining only walks it down slowly:
   **+5.4 % at dx = 8, +2.2 % at dx = 4, +1.1 % at dx = 2**. No grid this engine
   can afford removes it. Cost is in §7.

**Retire the boundary-layer story as an explanation of the verdict flips.** Keep
it as a small, quantified, second-order correction to absolute exchange-ON
concentrations. The real resolution problem is the 7.5 um vessel source and the
p99 statistic built on top of it.

## 2. The length scales that actually exist in this engine

Three, not one. Computed at `dx = 12 um` on the real Xenium GBM section and the
real Xenium breast section (`data_cache/xenium_breast_janesick/rep1`).

| symbol | definition | active when | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| `delta` | `sqrt(D_eff/(PS + clearance + binding))` | exchange **ON** only | only where `PS > 0` |
| `L` | `sqrt(D_eff/(clearance + binding))` | **always** (this is what ships) | everywhere in tissue |
| `R` | `VESSEL_RADIUS_UM = 7.5 um` | **always** | the source's own half-width |

**`PS` is binary, not smooth.** The shipped vascular field is
`_DEFAULT_VASCULAR_MODE = "discrete"`: `vascular_density` is **exactly 1.0** in a
source cell and **exactly 0.0** elsewhere (measured: min = median = max = 1.0 over
the 5,199 GBM / 15,191 breast perfused cells). So
`PS = bbb_penetrance * PS_SCALE_S_INV` inside a vessel and zero outside, and
`delta` is defined **only on 2.68 % (GBM) / 5.67 % (breast) of the tissue**.

### 2.2 Reading the census

* **The 15-17 um claim is confirmed.** Nine drugs across the two panels have
  `delta` between 14.46 and 16.66 um, and every one of them has
  `bbb_penetrance >= 0.5788`. The threshold quoted in
  `transvascular_exchange_revalidation.md` §5 is right.
* **`delta/dx = 1.21-1.39` at the shipped grid.** "On the order of one cell per
  boundary layer" is an accurate description.
* **`delta` exceeds the vessel RADIUS everywhere, and is comparable to the vessel
  DIAMETER.** The smallest single-voxel `delta` anywhere in either section is
  **10.12 um** (breast cyclophosphamide), against `R = 7.5 um` and `2R = 15 um`.
  So the reaction layer is never confined *inside* the vessel: the perivascular
  region is reaction-limited, not diffusion-limited, and there is no thin interior
  sub-layer for the mesh to resolve. The sharpest genuine feature in the field is
  the **source's own 7.5 um half-width**, which is sub-cell at every dx >= 8 um
  and is *drug independent*. (For the nine high-penetrance drugs the median
  `delta` of 14.46-16.66 um and the source width `2R = 15 um` are the SAME scale
  to within 11 %, which is why they cannot be separated on tissue and why §3 has
  to separate them by construction.)
* **`delta` and `L` order the drugs DIFFERENTLY.** `delta` is short exactly where
  `bbb_penetrance` is high; `L` is short exactly where `binding` is high. In the
  breast panel these are nearly anti-correlated. This is what makes question 2
  decidable.

### 2.3 Does a dx sweep on real tissue change the PHYSICS? (mostly no)

A 7.5 um-radius disc has area 176.7 um^2; one `dx = 24 um` cell has 576 um^2. That
looked like it would make a dx sweep on tissue a change of problem rather than a
refinement. **Measured, it largely does not**, because vessels are multi-cell
clusters whose painted union is set by cluster extent, not by single discs:

refinement in the aggregate. What is **not** preserved is the sub-cell SHAPE of
the source, and §4.4 shows that is where the damage is.

## 3. The controlled problem

Cartesian 1-D two-region slab, `Lx = 480 um`, no-flux at both ends, constant `D`:

* `0 <= x < a`: reaction `k_in`, source `S = PS*Cp`
* `a <= x <= Lx`: reaction `k_out = kappa`, no source

Exact steady state (`slab_exact`), matching value and flux at `x = a`:

```
[0, a) :  C = PS*Cp/k_in  +  A cosh(x/sqrt(D/k_in))
[a, Lx]:  C = B cosh((Lx - x)/sqrt(D/kappa))
```

`k_in = PS + kappa` is the exchange-ON law and `k_in = kappa` (same source) is the
one-way leak, so **one closed form scores both arms**. Verified against the ODE by
finite differences (max residual 4.1e-6 against a source term of order 0.58, away
from the interface) and both no-flux conditions hold to 1e-8.

`D = 140.41 um^2/s` (the shipping `D_tissue`) and `kappa = D/64.92^2` so the OUTER
length is pinned at the measured doxorubicin e-fold anchor **64.92 um** in every
series; `PS` is then set to hit each target `delta`. Within a series the only
thing that changes is `delta/dx`.

**The reference is cell-averaged, not point-sampled.** The scheme is finite
volume, so its unknowns approximate cell averages; each reference cell value is a
Simpson quadrature of the closed form over that cell (verified insensitive to the
sub-division count from 64 to 16384, agreeing to 1e-16). Comparing against point
samples would have injected an O(dx^2) error belonging to the comparison rather
than the scheme.

**Three geometries:**

| tag | `a` | why |
|---|---|---|
| `wide_aligned` | 48 um | an integer number of cells at every dx <= 24 -> interface exactly on a cell face -> **pure boundary-layer error** |
| `wide_offset` | 54 um | deliberately off-face at dx = 12/16/24 -> adds interface quantisation |
| `vessel` | **7.5 um** | the ACTUAL shipped source half-width |

**The temporal error is exactly zero.** Every solve is integrated to `t = 40 tau`
and the measured `steady_drift` (relative change in mean over the last save
interval) is **0.0 at every one of the 360 solves**. Backward Euler's fixed point
is the exact discrete steady state independent of `dt`, so **every error below is
purely spatial**.

**On dt and the CFL condition (the coordinator's note):** the slab runs with
`convection=False` (no velocity field), so the explicit upwind CFL
`dt(|vx|+|vy|)/dx <= 1` **does not apply** and cannot contaminate these numbers.
`dt` is recorded per point in `blayer_controlled.json` (`*_dt_s`, `*_n_steps`,
`*_convection`); for the tabulated series it is 32.82 s over 2000 steps at every
dx. The CFL question is priced separately, on real tissue, in §7.

### 4.2 The error law

Least squares over all 54 aligned points:

```
|rel err of mean|  =  1.4267e-3  *  dx^1.9388  *  delta^-1.1447        R^2 = 0.9950
```

* **`p = 1.94` -- clean second order in dx.** The scheme is not degraded by the
  reaction-rate discontinuity when the interface is on a cell face.
* **`q = -1.14`, not `-2`.** So the error is **NOT a function of `delta/dx` alone**.
  Halving both `delta` and `dx` together still increases the error. "Cells per
  boundary layer" is a useful shorthand but it is not the controlling group; the
  honest statement is *second order in dx with a coefficient that grows roughly as
  `1/delta`*.
* The remaining length in the problem (`L_out = 64.92 um`) was held fixed, so `C`
  absorbs it. Treat the law as valid for `L_out` near 65 um, `delta` in 5-45 um,
  `dx` in 1-24 um, interface aligned.

the same interface.

Two facts dominate everything else in this report:

1. **The `penetration_index` error is essentially IDENTICAL for exchange ON and
   OFF** (8.347 % vs 8.382 % at dx = 12; the gap is 0.4 % of the error). The

2. **With exchange OFF the domain MEAN has exactly zero grid error** -- max

   conservation identity, not a coincidence: the finite-volume operator `L` has
   zero row sums, so summing the steady state over cells gives
   `kappa * sum(C) = sum(S)` exactly, independent of `dx`. It **fails the moment
   `k` becomes spatially variable**, which is precisely what `exchange_k` does.

So the honest statement of what exchange-ON costs numerically is:
**it converts a quantity that was exactly grid-independent (the mean) into one
that is second-order convergent.** That is a real new resolution requirement --
it is just a small one.

## 5. Does `delta` predict which verdicts move? No -- `L` does

Correlated against the per-drug dx movement **already measured in this repo**
(nothing recomputed; the sibling agents own those sweeps).

### 5.1 GBM, exchange ON (`transvascular_exchange_revalidation.md` §5, dx 12 -> 8)

| drug | delta (um) | L (um) | measured movement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ribociclib | 15.13 | 69.7 | +2.61 % |
| lomustine | 15.74 | 112.9 | +1.90 % |
| procarbazine | 16.66 | 112.5 | +1.81 % |
| carmustine | 16.24 | 156.8 | +1.36 % |
| cisplatin | 16.08 | 160.6 | +1.33 % |
| doxorubicin (control) | 57.04 | 72.9 | +0.00 % |

**Spearman(|movement|, delta) = -0.771**; Spearman(|movement|, L) = -0.371.
Where exchange is genuinely ON, **`delta` does rank the movers**, with the right
sign (thinner layer -> bigger movement), and the flat doxorubicin control is
exactly the drug with the thickest layer.

**On magnitude, be careful.** The aligned slab predicts that refining 12 -> 8 um
raises the mean by `0.905 - 0.413` = **+0.49 %** at `delta = 15` (and +1.16 % at
`delta = 5`); the tissue moved **+1.3 to +2.6 %**. Same sign, same order, but the
slab does not account for the whole of it -- and the natural candidate does not
help, because the **`vessel`-geometry term moves the OTHER way over that
interval** (`+1.07 % -> -0.95 %`, i.e. **-2.0 %** from 12 to 8 at `delta = 15`).
So the residual is not explained by anything measured here. What the controlled
problem establishes is the **sign** of the boundary-layer term and its **order**
(sub-1 % at dx = 12), not a quantitative prediction of a 2-D section carrying
thousands of curved, mesh-unaligned vessel boundaries whose quantisation error is
non-monotone in dx (see the `wide_offset` and `vessel` rows). Claiming more than
that would repeat the original `transvascular_exchange_revalidation.md` §5

### 5.3 The mechanism that IS consistent with the breast flip

`kill_calibration.decompose` computes the scored 3-D:2-D ratio by dividing the
whole per-cell exposure vector by its **99th percentile**
(`BATH_REFERENCE_PCT = 99.0`) before the Hill map. The scored verdict is therefore
a **mean-over-p99 shape statistic** -- structurally the same family as
`penetration_index`, and §4.4 measures that family's grid error at **+8.4 % at
dx = 12 and +17.8 % at dx = 24**, versus **exactly 0 %** for the mean. A drug whose
field is spatially sharp (short `L`) has a larger gap between its mean and its
p99, so it is where that error bites hardest. That is a complete account of the
docetaxel movement that never mentions `delta`.

## 8. Recommendation

**For MEAN / AUC-type quantities under the exchange-ON law: `dx = 12 um`,
`dt = 10 s` is adequate and no change is needed.** The boundary layer costs
~0.9 % at the panel's minimum `delta` of 14.5 um, and the error law shows 1 %
needs only `dx <= 13.2 um`. The `transvascular_exchange_revalidation.md` §5
concern was real in mechanism and
over-stated in magnitude, and its directional half was simply wrong.

**Quote `delta/dx >= 1.1` as the requirement**, not the usual "several cells per
layer": measured, this scheme reaches 1 % at 1.09 cells per layer because the
reaction rate enters the implicit operator as a diagonal.

**For p99-normalised or distribution-shaped quantities -- which is EVERY 3-D:2-D
verdict, `penetration_index`, and anything scored through
`kill_calibration.decompose` -- `dx = 12 um` carries a ~8 % bias that refining to
dx = 8 um only reduces to +5.4 % (and dx = 4 um to +2.2 %).** This is a
source-geometry error set by the
7.5 um vessel radius, present identically with exchange on or off, and it is not
fixed by any dx this engine can afford. **Refining the grid is the wrong lever
here.** The right levers are (a) an area-weighted rather than binary vessel source
(so a sub-cell disc contributes its true area), or (b) reporting these verdicts
with an explicit grid-bias band rather than as point values. Both are outside this
report's scope; neither is a solver change.

**Do not spend compute chasing the boundary layer.** Spend it, if anywhere, on the
p99 normaliser.
