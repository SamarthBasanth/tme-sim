# Grid convergence of the breast 3-D:2-D kill-calibration panel — is `dx = 12 um` converged, and is docetaxel's FAIL real?

Generated by `experiments/grid_convergence_breast.py` on the same REAL Xenium breast section as `results/kill_calibration_breast.md` (`data_cache/xenium_breast_janesick/rep1`; Janesick *et al.* 2023, PMID 38114474), with the SAME `dt = 10 s`, the same 6 h window, the same `save_every = 10`, the same seed 0, the same `PDConfig`, the same shipping diffusivity, the same alpha = 148.6082, the same p99 bath-matching convention and the same frozen literature bands. **Only `dx` is varied.** 42 transport solves over 7 grids x 6 drugs, 541 min.

> **This is a convergence audit, not a calibration.** No grid was selected by the verdict it produces. The convergence curve is reported as measured and the conclusion follows from it, whichever way it falls.

> ### STALE TRANSPORT WARNING — every rung except `dx = 12 um` predates the `_TUMOR_SIGMA` units fix
>
> The interstitial-pressure solve smooths the tumour-occupancy field with a gaussian whose width was, until commit `5d6e8e4`, expressed in GRID CELLS (`_TUMOR_SIGMA = 5.0`) and so tracked the mesh — 40 um at `dx = 8`, 60 at 12, 80 at 16, 120 at 24. It is now a physical length (`_TUMOR_SIGMA_UM = 60.0`). **That is precisely the defect a grid-convergence study cannot tolerate**: refining the mesh was silently redefining the model, so the ladder below measures discretisation error and a model change at once. `results/tumor_sigma_units.md` §3 measures it on the mean tissue IFP: **1.89x spread across the ladder before, 1.09x after**.
>
> **`dx = 12 um` is the exact fixed point** (`60.0 / 12.0 == 5.0` in IEEE-754), so the published rung, the §1 control and the §1c cache comparison are bit-identical and unaffected. The other rungs (8, 10, 16, 18, 24, 30 um) were solved under the old constant and **have NOT been re-solved here** — this regeneration re-scored stored ratios against the corrected literature bands and solved no PDE (`results/fallback_rule_propagation.md`). The verdicts below are correct for the ratios shown; the ratios at every rung but 12 would move by an unmeasured amount on a re-run, and with them the Richardson and power-law extrapolations. **The docetaxel conclusion should be re-derived from a re-solved ladder before it is quoted again.** Re-solving costs 42 transport solves (~541 min) and was deliberately not done here.

## 1b. The CFL question — `dt` is held FIXED at 10 s on every rung, and that is measured to be safe HERE

A separate audit flagged that **`dx = 10 um` violates the advection CFL condition at `dt = 10 s`**. That statement is true and is recorded in `results/delivery_metrics.md` — but it is about the **GBM** section (40,887 cells, grid (424, 675) at dx = 12 um), whose interstitial velocity field is faster. It does not transfer to this breast section, and the difference is measured, not assumed.

Two facts make the fine rungs safe here:

1. **The solver's CFL check is a hard `raise`, not a warning** (`tmesim/physics/diffusion.py`: `if cfl > 1.0: raise ValueError(...)`). It is evaluated from the same `max(|vx|+|vy|)` reported in §2. A run that violated the condition could not have produced a number at all — it would have aborted. Every rung below completed, so every rung satisfied `CFL <= 1`.
2. **The measured CFL on THIS section**, at `dt = 10 s`: **0.809** at dx = 8 um, **0.816** at dx = 10 um, **0.495** at dx = 12 um, **0.334** at dx = 16 um, **0.307** at dx = 18 um, **0.261** at dx = 24 um, **0.182** at dx = 30 um. Probed one grid finer, `dx = 6 um` gives max speed 0.9419 um/s and **CFL = 1.570**, and the solver refuses. **`dx = 8 um` is therefore the finest grid reachable at the published `dt`**, and that is the stated scope limit of this study.

**`dt` was NOT scaled with `dx`.** That is deliberate: a ladder that changes `dt` with `dx` measures the SUM of the spatial and temporal discretisation errors and cannot attribute the movement to the mesh, which is the entire question here. ## 1c. There is no grid-error cancellation in the scored ratio — verified

It is tempting to assume the 3-D:2-D ratio is a ratio of two runs whose mesh errors partly cancel. It is not. `kill_calibration.decompose` computes the denominator as `kill_2d = p_hill(c_bath, ic50, hill) * mean(sens)` — a SCALAR Hill evaluation with no mesh in it at all. Measured across every record in this study, `kill_2d_model` takes exactly `0.5` and `f_sensitivity` exactly `1` — i.e. the scored ratio is simply `2 x kill_3d`, a fully grid-dependent numerator over a grid-free constant.

What DOES cancel is multiplicative SCALE error: `plasma = c_bath / p99(e_unit)` re-solves the plasma level so the p99 tumour cell sits exactly at the bath concentration, so any change that rescales the whole exposure field uniformly cancels exactly. A mesh change does not rescale the field — it changes its SHAPE, sharpening or smearing the perivascular gradient, which is precisely the thing the ratio is built to be sensitive to. **The scored ratio should therefore be expected to be MORE grid-sensitive than the absolute exposure, not less**, and §3 measures exactly that.
