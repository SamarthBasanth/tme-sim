# Grid convergence of the breast 3-D:2-D kill-calibration panel — is `dx = 12 um` converged, and is docetaxel's FAIL real?

Generated by `experiments/grid_convergence_breast.py` on the same REAL Xenium breast section as `results/kill_calibration_breast.md` (`data_cache/xenium_breast_janesick/rep1`; Janesick *et al.* 2023, PMID 38114474), with the SAME `dt = 10 s`, the same 6 h window, the same `save_every = 10`, the same seed 0, the same `PDConfig`, the same shipping diffusivity, the same alpha = 148.6082, the same p99 bath-matching convention and the same frozen literature bands. **Only `dx` is varied.** 42 transport solves over 7 grids x 6 drugs, 542 min.

> **This is a convergence audit, not a calibration.** No grid was selected by the verdict it produces. The convergence curve is reported as measured and the conclusion follows from it, whichever way it falls.

## 0. Bottom line

**Docetaxel.** Published `dx = 12 um` ratio **0.1484** vs band top edge **0.1463** — FAIL by 1.48 %. On the finest grid the solver will run at the published `dt` (`dx = 8 um`) the ratio is **0.1521**. Richardson-extrapolated to `dx -> 0`: **0.1537** (FAIL); whole-ladder power-law fit: **0.1573** (FAIL).

**Verdict stability over the ladder.** 5/6 verdicts are constant at every grid; 1 flip somewhere on the ladder (**docetaxel**).

**Grid limit.** The explicit upwind advection step carries the CFL condition `dt*(|vx|+|vy|)/dx <= 1` and the solver RAISES if it is violated. On this section at `dt = 10 s` the measured CFL is 0.809 at dx = 8 um, 0.816 at dx = 10 um, 0.495 at dx = 12 um, 0.334 at dx = 16 um, 0.307 at dx = 18 um, 0.261 at dx = 24 um, 0.182 at dx = 30 um. At `dx = 6 um` it is **1.570** and the solver refuses, so **`dx = 8 um` is the finest grid reachable at the published `dt`** — going finer would require also cutting `dt` and would confound spatial with temporal discretisation error.

## 2. The ladder — what changes with dx besides the PDE

## 5. The boundary-layer hypothesis — TESTED, not asserted

The hypothesis on the table was that the drugs whose perivascular boundary layer `L = sqrt(D_eff/(binding + clearance))` is thin relative to the cell size are the ones whose ratio is mesh-sensitive. That is a prediction with a sign and a magnitude, so it is testable against the measured ladder. `L/dx` at the published grid, against the measured total spread of the ratio over the ladder:

| docetaxel | 28.2 | 2.4 | 49.7 % | 3.4 % |
| paclitaxel | 28.8 | 2.4 | 48.5 % | 3.1 % |
| doxorubicin | 64.9 | 5.4 | 12.8 % | — % |
| epirubicin | 64.9 | 5.4 | 12.8 % | — % |
| cisplatin | 142.9 | 11.9 | 1.6 % | — % |
| 5-fluorouracil | 209.9 | 17.5 | 4.1 % | — % |

Rank/log correlation between `L` and the ladder spread: **r = -0.930** (n = 6).
