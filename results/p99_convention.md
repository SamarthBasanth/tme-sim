# Is p99 bath-matching the right normalising convention, or just *a* convention?

Generated by `experiments/p99_convention.py` (stages A-G). Raw records:
`results/sweep/p99_algebra.json`, `p99_gridslab.json`, `p99_dtissue_dx12.json`
(+ `.jsonl`), `p99_verdicts.json`, `p99_stability.json`, `p99_perivascular.json`,
`p99_transfer.json`.

**The question.** `tmesim/validation/kill_calibration.decompose` sets the plasma
level so that the **99th percentile** of the per-tumour-cell exposure field sits
exactly at the bath concentration, then applies the Hill map. Three independent
investigations converged on the consequence — `results/boundary_layer_resolution.md`
§4.4/§5.3 (the `mean/p99` shape statistic carries **+8.3 % at dx = 12 um** against
an exact solution, while the domain mean carries **exactly zero**),
`results/dtissue_sensitivity.md` §5/§7.3 (a **3.07x** change in mean tumour
exposure moves the scored ratio by **0.997-1.009x**), and
`results/grid_convergence_gbm.md` §7 (halving dt moves the exposure field by
1.2e-04 and the score by 0.0003 %). All three explain it the same way: p99
bath-matching removes multiplicative SCALE error exactly, so only SHAPE error
survives. **Nobody had asked whether 99 is the right number** — and, as §3.5
shows, nobody had checked whether the +8.3 % actually transfers from the slab to
the tissue verdicts it has been applied to. It does not.

---

## 0. Bottom line

1. **The scale-invariance is exact, and it is NOT a property of p99.** Measured
   to machine precision (worst deviation **6.7e-16** over 9 drugs x 8 scale
   factors spanning 1e-6 to 1e6): the scored ratio is invariant under
   `e -> lambda*e` for **every** normaliser that is positively homogeneous of
   degree 1 — p50, p90, p95, p99, p99.9, max, mean, rms, L4, the
   exposure-weighted mean, and the conditional tail means. The protection comes
   from *normalising by a degree-1-homogeneous statistic of the field itself*,
   not from the choice of 99. A fixed physical reference (the negative control)
   moves the score by **2.00x to 19.3x** under the same perturbation.

2. **Because the domain mean is grid-exact, the entire grid error of the scored
   ratio IS the grid error of the normaliser.** With exchange OFF the domain mean
   is protected by a discrete conservation identity, so on the controlled slab
   `err(mean/N) = -err(N)/(1 + err(N))` holds to two decimals at every one of the
   40 points (e.g. p99 at dx = 12: normaliser -7.73 %, statistic +8.38 %). The
   convention is not one contributor to the grid-error budget; at leading order
   it is the *whole* budget.

3. **The choice of 99 was never justified, and the one written justification
   does not reach it.** The only argument in the repo is "p99 is used rather than
   the max so a single grid cell cannot set the reference" (`kill_calibration.py`
   line 137-141, echoed in `docs/MATHEMATICAL_MODEL.md` §3.8). That argues
   against `max`. It does not argue for 99 over 95, 90, or a tail mean. Nothing
   in the git history, the results files, or the docs compares them. **An
   undocumented constant sets the robustness of every scored verdict in the
   repo.**

4. **p99 is measurably above its own stated physical referent.** The convention's
   stated meaning is "the best-perfused cells, the analogue of a spheroid's outer
   surface". Measured on the real sections: the tumour cells that actually sit in
   a **perfused voxel** have a mean exposure at the **p67.9-p98.1** of the
   distribution (median across the 9 drug/tissue pairs: **p95.2**), and the
   spread is drug-dependent (5-fluorouracil p67.9, docetaxel p97.0) because the
   decay length differs per drug. And on the literature side of the same
   analogy, a spheroid's outer one-cell shell is **17-66 %** of its cells for
   radii 250-50 um. p99 is a fixed number standing in for a quantity that is
   neither fixed nor near 99.

5. **A strictly better convention exists inside the same physical idea.**
   `tail90` = **the mean exposure of the best-perfused 10 % of tumour cells**
   (a conditional tail mean / CVaR-90) keeps the upper-tail meaning but is an
   *integral over a shell* rather than a single order statistic. On the same
   controlled problem at the shipped dx = 12 um it carries **-0.08 %** where p99
   carries **+6.98 %** — an **85x** reduction — with **half** p99's bootstrap
   variance and no detectable half-sample bias.

6. **Verdict consequences, computed last and never used to select** (§5):
   `tail90` reproduces the published verdict set **exactly** — 6/7 PASS, the same
   6 and the same 1 FAIL. Nothing is bought or lost at the verdict level. (Nine rows
   are swept; two of them — breast paclitaxel and 5-fluorouracil — carry no verdict
   under any convention, having one drug-matched study each and therefore a zero-width
   band. See §5.)

7. **The slab's "+8.3 % at dx = 12" does NOT transfer to tissue, and it has
   been read as though it does.** `results/boundary_layer_resolution.md` §8 says
   dx = 12 um carries that bias for "every 3-D:2-D verdict in this repo".
   Measured like-for-like (the same dx = 12 -> 8 step on both), the slab predicts
   a uniform **+2.39 %** while the nine tissue ratios move **-2.43 % to
   +3.79 %** — a 6.2-point, drug-ordered spread — and the two shortest-`L` drugs
   (paclitaxel, docetaxel) move in the **opposite direction**. The slab number is
   a correct measurement of the *scoring statistic's* error in isolation and is
   the right instrument for ranking conventions (§3.1); it is **not** a bias that
   can be subtracted from a tissue verdict (§3.5). That same file's §6 already
   gives the reason — on Xenium tissue "something dx-dependent in the
   reconstruction dominates" — so its §6 and its §8 are in tension, and §6 is the
   one that is right.

8. **Exactly one of the seven scored verdicts is grid-sensitive at all: breast
   docetaxel**, the repo's only FAIL, whose margin to its band is **+1.5 %**
   against grid movements of 2.4-7.0 % from every available estimate — which
   disagree in sign with each other. The other six carry **33.8 %-1991 %**
   margins and are untouchable; the remaining two rows have no band and so no
   margin to be grid-sensitive in. **The honest verdict is UNDETERMINED at
   dx = 12 um** — not FAIL, and emphatically not PASS (§6).

**Recommendation (§7): change the convention to `tail90`, and state the residual
bias.** Cost is ~2.5-3.5 h of compute and seven report regenerations. If that is not
affordable now, the minimum honest alternative is to keep p99 and publish the
**tissue-measured** grid band (-2.4 % to +3.8 %, drug-dependent) alongside every
scored ratio — but the docetaxel FAIL must be downgraded to UNDETERMINED either
way, and the +8.3 % transfer claim withdrawn either way.

## 1. What p99 bath-matching does, algebraically

### 1.1 The statement

`decompose` (`kill_calibration.py` 520-544) does, in order:

```
ref     = N(e)                     # N = the 99th percentile
plasma  = c_bath / ref
E_i     = plasma * e_i  =  c_bath * e_i / N(e)
p3_i    = p_hill(E_i ; IC50, h)
ratio   = mean_i[p3_i * s_i * r_i] / (p_hill(c_bath) * mean_i[s_i])
```

The exposure field `e` enters **only** through `E = c_bath * e / N(e)`. Write
`u = e / N(e)`. Then for any normaliser `N` that is **positively homogeneous of
degree 1**, i.e. `N(lambda*e) = lambda*N(e)` for all `lambda > 0`:

```
u(lambda*e) = lambda*e / N(lambda*e) = lambda*e / (lambda*N(e)) = u(e)
```

so `E`, `p3`, and therefore `ratio` are **identically unchanged**. This holds:

* **exactly**, not to leading order — there is no small parameter;
* **independently of the Hill nonlinearity**, because the normalisation happens
  *before* the Hill map, so the nonlinearity never sees the scale factor;
* **independently of `sens` and `resist`**, which do not touch `e` at all;
* for **every** `N` in the homogeneous family, not for p99 specifically.

Every percentile (including the max and the median), every `L^p` norm, the mean,
the exposure-weighted mean `mean(e^2)/mean(e)`, and every conditional tail mean
is degree-1 homogeneous. **The scale-invariance the three prior reports
attribute to p99 is a property of the normalisation step, not of the statistic
chosen.** That reframing matters, because it means the property is free — it
costs nothing to keep while choosing a better `N`.

### 1.2 Verified, not asserted (stage A1)

Real published exposure fields, 9 drug/tissue pairs, scaled by
`1e-6, 1e-3, 0.1, 0.5, 2, 10, 1e3, 1e6`. Worst `|relative change in ratio_exact|`:

| convention | worst rel. change | | convention | worst rel. change |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| p50 | 2.2e-16 | | mean | 5.6e-16 |
| p90 | 4.4e-16 | | rms (L2) | 4.4e-16 |
| p95 | 3.3e-16 | | L4 | 6.7e-16 |
| p99 **(shipped)** | 4.4e-16 | | exposure-weighted mean | 4.4e-16 |
| p99.9 | 4.4e-16 | | tail90 / tail95 / tail99 | 4.4 / 5.6 / 3.3e-16 |
| max | 4.4e-16 | | **fixed reference (control)** | **2.00x-19.3x** |

Machine epsilon is 2.2e-16. Every homogeneous convention is exact to 1-3 ulp;
the non-homogeneous control fails by up to a factor of 19.

### 1.3 What is destroyed, and what is merely preserved

**Removed exactly** — a *uniform multiplicative* error in the exposure field.
That covers: a wrong unit, a wrong plasma scale, a wrong `bbb_penetrance`, a
wrong source magnitude, and any error entering as a global factor on `AUC`. This
is why halving `dt` moves the field 1.2e-04 and the score 3e-6
(`grid_convergence_gbm.md` §7) — a `dt` change is very nearly a pure rescaling.

**Preserved in full** — anything that changes the *shape* of the exposure
distribution, i.e. the vector `u = e/N(e)`:
* the ratio of `N(e)` to the rest of the distribution (this is the grid error,
  §3.1);
* spatially varying multiplicative error (stage A3: 2 % iid multiplicative noise
  moves the score by up to **1.6e-02** under `max`, **5.3e-03** under p99,
  **1.6e-03** under `tail90` and **1.4e-04** under the mean — the ordering is the
  same one every later section reproduces);
* the composition of the cell set the percentile is taken over (a percentile is
  a property of the *sample*, so which cells are tumour cells is now an input to
  the normaliser — §3.3);
* any change in the sharpness of the field, which is exactly what refining `dx`
  and changing `D_tissue` in `frozen` mode do.

**Outside the protection entirely** — every ABSOLUTE quantity: `mean_auc`,
`penetration_index` (itself p99-normalised, so it is *inside* a different
instance of the same problem), coverage, penetration depth, and absolute kill
fraction. `dtissue_sensitivity.md` §5 measures this gap directly: in `recal`
mode the scored ratio moves 0.977-1.004x while the absolute kill at a real dose
moves 0.580-0.758x. **The ratio being stable licenses nothing about the field.**

### 1.4 The consequence nobody had drawn: the convention *is* the grid-error budget

`boundary_layer_resolution.md` §4.4 established that with exchange OFF the
domain mean has exactly zero grid error (a discrete conservation identity: the
finite-volume operator has zero row sums, so `kappa*sum(C) = sum(S)` independent
of `dx`). Reproduced here: max `|rel err|` of the domain mean over 20 slab points
with exchange OFF is **6.2e-06**, which is the quadrature error of *this* report's
4e5-point continuum reference, not of the scheme (measured against the
cell-averaged reference in `blayer`, the scheme's own figure is 2.1e-11). With
exchange ON the same statistic reaches **10.2 %**, exactly as that report
predicted, because `k` becomes spatially variable and the identity breaks.

Combine that with §1.1. In the low-dose (linear-Hill) limit the scored delivery
factor is `f_pen -> mean(e)/N(e)`. If `mean` is grid-exact then

```
rel_err( mean/N )  =  -rel_err(N) / (1 + rel_err(N))   ~  -rel_err(N)
```

Measured at dx = 12 um on the real vessel geometry, exchange OFF:

| convention | rel. err of `N` itself | rel. err of `f_pen` at 1xIC50 |
|---|---:|---:|
| p50 | +0.731 % | -0.111 % |
| p90 | -7.077 % | +5.148 % |
| p95 | -8.048 % | +6.669 % |
| **p99 (shipped)** | **-7.734 %** | **+6.978 %** |
| p99.9 | -4.061 % | +3.575 % |
| max | -3.447 % | +3.020 % |
| **tail90** | **+0.108 %** | **-0.082 %** |
| tail95 | +0.382 % | -0.315 % |
| tail99 | -2.723 % | +2.366 % |
| mean | -0.001 % | +0.049 % |
| rms (L2) | -0.010 % | +0.035 % |
| exposure-weighted mean | -0.019 % | +0.024 % |

The mirroring is exact to the second decimal. **On the shipped exchange-OFF path
the grid error of the scored ratio is, to leading order, nothing but the grid
error of the chosen normaliser.** Choosing the normaliser therefore *is* choosing
the grid accuracy of every scored verdict, and it is a free choice in the sense
that the scale protection is unaffected (§1.2).

## 2. Was the choice ever justified?

**Read, in full: the only written justification in the repository.**

`tmesim/validation/kill_calibration.py`, lines 137-141, the definition site:

> *Percentile of the tumour-cell exposure distribution used as the "bath
> equivalent": in a spheroid assay the outer surface sits at the bath
> concentration, and the best-perfused cells in tissue are the analogue. p99 is
> used rather than the max so a single grid cell cannot set the reference; both
> are reported.*

`docs/MATHEMATICAL_MODEL.md` §3.8, for the sibling `penetration_index`:

> *The 99th percentile (rather than the max) is a robustness choice against
> single-cell outliers.*

That is the whole of it. **Both sentences argue `p99` against `max`. Neither
argues 99 against 95, 90, 80, or a tail mean**, and no file anywhere in the repo
compares them.

**Git archaeology.** `BATH_REFERENCE_PCT = 99.0` enters in a single commit,
`0eca2fc` (2026-07-28, "Time-resolved PK, 2D->3D kill validation, endothelial
audit, regeneration debt paid"), already at its final value. It has never been
changed. Five later commits touch the symbol (`d45f1b4`, `f69e468`, `ad542b4`,
`88c7882`, `9956b46`); every one of them *uses* it, none revisits it. The
introducing commit message does discuss the convention — honourably:

> *Also reported that the bath-matching convention flips cisplatin outside its
> band — per drug, rather than picking the flattering convention.*

So the repo knew from day one that the convention is load-bearing, reported it
per drug rather than hiding it, and never went back to ask which value is right.
`results/kill_calibration.md` limitation 11 and §3 item 5 say exactly this in the
published text.

**Verdict on question 2: the choice was never justified.** It was chosen, its
sensitivity was honestly disclosed at the p95/max level, and the disclosure was
then treated as sufficient. It is not sufficient, because §1.4 shows the constant
is not a reporting detail — it sets the numerical accuracy of the whole scored
apparatus.

## 3. The comparison across conventions

Twelve candidates in three estimator classes, plus a non-homogeneous control:

| class | members | what they are |
|---|---|---|
| order statistic | p50, p90, p95, **p99**, p99.9, max | one quantile of the cell distribution |
| conditional tail mean | tail90, tail95, tail99 | the MEAN over the top 10 / 5 / 1 % of cells (CVaR) |
| moment | mean, rms (L2), L4, `mean(e^2)/mean(e)` | a smooth integral functional of the field |
| control | fixed physical reference | no normalisation; the frozen published p99 value |

**Selection is made in §3.1-3.5 on numerical and statistical grounds only.
Verdict consequences are §5 and were computed afterwards.**

### 3.1 Grid error at the shipped grid (the controlled problem)

shipped source). Exchange OFF, which is the law the published verdicts ran on

**What the mechanism is, stated so it transfers.** The grid error of a normaliser
is governed by **how much of its support lies in the sub-cell region the mesh
cannot resolve**. The source half-width is 7.5 um against `dx = 12 um`. On the
slab, the top 1 % of the domain by length is `x < 4.8 um` — *entirely* inside one
grid cell, so `p99` is read off a single unresolved cell average and under-reads
by 7.7 %. `tail90` averages over the top 10 %, i.e. `x < 48 um` = four cells, most
of which are resolved, so the unresolved part is diluted 4:1 and the error falls
to 0.1 %. The mean is protected outright by the conservation identity.

That criterion is checkable on the real sections and it is not favourable to p99:
**perfused voxels are 2.68 % (GBM) / 5.67 % (breast) of tissue area**
(`boundary_layer_resolution.md` §2), so a top-**1 %** statistic sits entirely
inside the unresolved perivascular region on both, a top-5 % statistic sits at its
edge, and a top-10 % statistic extends past it.

**Convergence behaviour, not just magnitude.** Over the whole ladder the order
statistics p99.9 and max are **non-monotone** in dx (p99.9: +0.41 % at dx = 3,
+0.45 % at 4, +1.04 % at 6, +0.90 % at 8) and, under the population-sampled
variant below, p90/p95/p99 become wildly non-monotone (`mean/p95` in the vessel
geometry: +8.73 % at dx = 12, +0.11 % at 16, +17.56 % at 24, **-12.94 %** at 30).
This is structural: a quantile of a field represented by `nx` numbers is a **step
function of dx**, because refining moves *which* cell the quantile lands in.
**That is a complete explanation of a finding this repo already has and could not
account for** — `results/grid_convergence_gbm.md` §3 reports the GBM panel as
"NON-monotone, oscillating 6.6-7.4 % below dx = 18 with no extractable order of
accuracy", and its power-law fit is flagged DEGENERATE. An order statistic does
not have an order of accuracy. A tail mean or a moment does.

**Two samplings, both reported.** The table above takes each statistic over the
*grid cells*. The pipeline instead nearest-neighbour samples the grid field at a
fixed cell set (`domain.to_index(cells.x, cells.y)`), so a second variant lays
20,000 dx-independent "cells" on the slab and re-scores. The two bracket the real
case (breast: 89,785 tumour cells over 267,838 tissue grid cells, i.e. 0.34
cells per grid cell; GBM 23,812 cells). Under population sampling at dx = 12 the
vessel-geometry `f_pen` errors are p90 +5.14 %, p95 +6.66 %, **p99 +1.05 %**,
p99.9 +3.00 %, max +3.02 %, **tail90 -0.08 %**, tail95 -0.31 %, mean +0.05 %.
p99 looks much better under this sampling **at this one dx** and much worse at
others (-1.59 % at dx = 8, +9.32 % at 24) — the step-function behaviour again.
`tail90` and the moments are insensitive to which sampling is used. **The
ranking that survives both samplings is: moments ~ tail90 << tail95 < everything
else.**

### 3.2 Sensitivity to the diffusivity perturbation

Same perturbation `results/dtissue_sensitivity.md` §7.3 ran on the published
grid: the real breast section at **dx = 12 um**, D_tissue from the shipping
1.404e-06 to **4.559e-07 cm^2/s**, both modes, using that file's own `build_drug`
and `e_unit_for` so the arm is identical. The reference arm is the published run
cache, asserted per drug against the `_e_unit_sum` / `_e_unit_first` fingerprints
in `results/sweep/dtissue_confirm_dx12.json`; the 12 perturbed fields are fresh
solves, each asserted not bit-identical to its reference.

**Reproduction check.** At `tag = p99` this returns 0.7593 / 0.6821 / 0.6807 /
0.7593 / 0.8657 / 0.8269 (`frozen`) and 1.0044 / 1.0088 / 1.0085 / 1.0044 /
0.9970 / 0.9977 (`recal`), against that file's published 0.759 / 0.682 / 0.681 /
0.759 / 0.866 / 0.827 and 1.004 / 1.009 / 1.009 / 1.004 / 0.997 / 0.998. Same
numbers.

The two modes are different tests and must be read differently:

* **`recal` is the mode the codebase actually runs** (`calibrate_alpha()` is
  called at run time). D changes, `alpha` is re-solved against the SAME measured
  doxorubicin penetration anchor, so the field's *measured* physical content —
  the 64.92 um e-fold length — is **unchanged**. Mean tumour exposure moves
  **2.96x-3.09x**. The correct response of a scored ratio is therefore
  approximately **nothing**, and how much leaks through is a pure defect measure.
* **`frozen`** keeps the published `alpha`, so `L` moves as `sqrt(D)` and the
  field shape genuinely changes. A response here is *correct*; a convention that
  does not respond is blind to the thing the model exists to measure.

| p95 | 0.0055 | 0.2818 |

Three readings:

1. **The cancellation `dtissue_sensitivity.md` found is confirmed and is again
   not a property of p99.** Every homogeneous convention absorbs a 3.07x exposure
   change to within 2.1 %; the non-homogeneous control moves by 1.90x-2.39x. §1.1
   says this must be so, and the residual is nonzero only because `recal` is not
   a *pure* rescaling — re-solving `alpha` also changes the sink field's shape a
   little.
2. **The residual ordering is the same ordering as everything else in this
   report.** The further into the tail the statistic sits, the more of the
   non-change leaks into the score: max 2.1 % > p99.9 1.5 % > tail99 1.2 % >
   **p99 0.9 %** > tail95 0.8 % > **tail90 0.6 %** > p95 0.6 % > rms 0.3 % >
   mean 0.14 % > p50 0.09 %. `tail90` passes **31 % less** of the spurious
   response than p99.
3. **And it supplies an independent reason to reject the low-order
   conventions.** In `frozen`, where the perivascular gradient genuinely changes,
   p50 registers **0.0097** and the mean **0.1279**, against 0.30-0.42 for every
   upper-tail statistic — i.e. the median is ~40x less sensitive than p99 to a
   real change in penetration. A convention that ignores parameter error by
   ignoring the physics is not robust, it is deaf. `tail90` keeps **79 %** of
   p99's `frozen` response (in log terms) while passing 31 % less of the
   spurious one.

**Caveat, stated rather than assumed.** `frozen` has no ground truth: there is no
independent answer for how far the score *should* move when the field shape
changes, so the second column ranks *sensitivity*, not *accuracy*. The only
ground-truth-referenced accuracy measure in this report is §3.1.

### 3.3 Estimator stability (stage E)

A normaliser is estimated from a finite cell set. On the real sections the top
1 % is **239 cells (GBM)** and **898 cells (breast)**; the top 0.1 % is **24** and
**88**. 200 bootstrap resamples and 200 half-samples of the tumour-cell set,
scoring the full ratio each time:

| convention | bootstrap CV of the score, % (min-max over the 9 drug/tissue pairs) | half-sample BIAS vs the full-sample score, % (worst) |
|---|---|---:|
| p50 | 0.103 - 0.235 | -0.030 |
| p90 | 0.101 - 0.407 | -0.043 |
| p95 | 0.118 - 0.631 | +0.067 |
| **p99 (shipped)** | **0.143 - 1.722** | **-0.199** |
| p99.9 | 0.281 - 2.060 | **+0.925** |
| max | 0.333 - 2.560 | **+2.966** |
| **tail90** | **0.107 - 0.757** | **-0.019** |
| tail95 | 0.122 - 1.112 | -0.040 |
| tail99 | 0.165 - 2.373 | +0.214 |

* **`max` and `p99.9` are not consistent estimators.** Halving the sample changes
  the *expected* score by up to **+2.97 %** (max, breast docetaxel/paclitaxel)
  and **+0.93 %** (p99.9, GBM cisplatin). A statistic whose expectation depends
  on how many cells the section happens to contain cannot be a scoring
  convention across tissues of different cellularity. This disqualifies both on
  statistical grounds alone, independently of §5.
* **`tail90` has roughly half `p99`'s sampling variance** (worst case 0.757 % vs
  1.722 %) and a tenth of its half-sample bias, while sitting in the same part of
  the distribution.

### 3.4 What is the physical bath analogue, measured (stage F)

The convention's stated meaning is structural: *the best-perfused cells are the
analogue of a spheroid's outer surface.* Both halves of that analogy are
measurable.

**The tissue half.** The shipped vascular field is binary, so "sits in a perfused
voxel" is exactly defined. Measured on the published sections at dx = 12 um:
**190 / 23,812 GBM tumour cells (0.80 %)** and **2,752 / 89,785 breast tumour
cells (3.07 %)** are in a perfused voxel. The percentile at which those cells'
**mean** exposure falls on the full distribution:

| tissue | drug | perivascular-mean sits at |
|---|---|---:|
| GBM | temozolomide | **p90.1** |
| GBM | cisplatin | p95.2 |
| GBM | doxorubicin | p98.1 |
| breast | doxorubicin | p91.8 |
| breast | paclitaxel | p96.9 |
| breast | docetaxel | p97.0 |
| breast | epirubicin | p91.8 |
| breast | 5-fluorouracil | **p67.9** |
| breast | cisplatin | p77.7 |

**The spheroid half.** The fraction of a spheroid's cells lying within one cell
diameter of the surface is `1 - (1 - d/R)^3`; for a 15 um cell that is **65.7 %**
at R = 50 um, **38.6 %** at 100, **27.1 %** at 150, **16.9 %** at 250. The bands
in `kill_calibration.py` come from 100-500 um spheroids, so "the outer surface
sits at the bath" refers to between a sixth and two thirds of the assayed cells.

**Both halves say the same thing: 99 is too high, and no single fixed percentile
is right, because the correct percentile is drug-dependent (p67.9 to p98.1, a
30-percentile spread driven by how fast each drug's field decays from a vessel).**
A tail *mean* over a fixed cell fraction is a better match to both halves of the
analogy than a fixed quantile: it is a shell, it averages, and it does not
pretend the tissue's decay length is the same for 5-fluorouracil and docetaxel
(`L` = 209.9 vs 28.2 um per `results/grid_convergence_breast.md`; 224.0 vs
30.1 um under `boundary_layer_resolution.md`'s median-`D_eff` convention — the
two files differ slightly and the ratio, ~7.5x, is what matters here).

### 3.5 Does the slab's +8.3 % transfer to tissue? **No — and that contradicts how it has been read**

This is the most consequential negative result in the report, so it is stated
plainly rather than left as a caveat.

`results/boundary_layer_resolution.md` §0.6 and §8 report the p99-normalised
shape statistic as carrying **"+8.3 % at dx = 12 um"** and say this applies to
*"any p99-normalised or distribution-shaped quantity — which includes every
3-D:2-D verdict in this repo"*. That sentence has since been used, in this
repo's commit messages and in the brief for this investigation, as though it
were a repo-wide bias that could be subtracted from a published tissue verdict.
**It cannot be.**

**The like-for-like test** (stage G, `results/sweep/p99_transfer.json`; the
tissue values are PARSED from the two published ladder reports, which sibling
workstreams own, and nothing in them is recomputed). The slab is solved at
dx = 12 and dx = 8; the tissue ladders in `results/grid_convergence_gbm.md` and
`results/grid_convergence_breast.md` also solve both. So the commensurable
quantity is the *same dx step* on both, `ratio(dx=12) / ratio(dx=8) - 1`. The
slab prediction is drug-independent by construction — one geometry, one field —
and equals **+2.39 %** at 1xIC50 and **+3.18 %** at temozolomide's 0.165xIC50.

| tissue | drug | `L` (um) | ratio @ dx = 12 | ratio @ dx = 8 | **tissue, 12 vs 8** | slab predicts | sign |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| GBM | temozolomide | 264.5 | 0.2626 | 0.2574 | **+2.02 %** | +3.18 % | same |
| GBM | cisplatin | 142.9 | 0.2791 | 0.2689 | **+3.79 %** | +2.39 % | same |
| GBM | doxorubicin | 64.9 | 0.2077 | 0.2049 | **+1.37 %** | +2.39 % | same |
| breast | doxorubicin | 64.9 | 0.2301 | 0.2296 | **+0.22 %** | +2.39 % | same |
| breast | epirubicin | 64.9 | 0.2301 | 0.2296 | **+0.22 %** | +2.39 % | same |
| breast | 5-fluorouracil | 209.9 | 0.3379 | 0.3354 | **+0.75 %** | +2.39 % | same |
| breast | cisplatin | 142.9 | 0.3085 | 0.3040 | **+1.48 %** | +2.39 % | same |
| breast | **paclitaxel** | **28.8** | 0.1503 | 0.1537 | **-2.21 %** | +2.39 % | **OPPOSITE** |
| breast | **docetaxel** | **28.2** | 0.1484 | 0.1521 | **-2.43 %** | +2.39 % | **OPPOSITE** |

And against the extrapolated limit rather than dx = 8, where
`grid_convergence_breast.md` §5 has Richardson estimates, the disagreement is
larger: the slab says dx = 12 reads **+6.98 %** high, the tissue says docetaxel
reads **-3.4 %** low and paclitaxel **-3.1 %** low.

**Four conclusions, in order of how well they are supported.**

1. **The slab error is drug-independent; the tissue error is not.** The slab must
   give one number — it has no drug identity, only a geometry. The tissue spans
   **-2.43 % to +3.79 %** over the same dx step, a **6.2-percentage-point** spread
   ordered by decay length `L`. **No single figure, +8.3 % or otherwise, describes
   the tissue's grid dependence.**
2. **For 7 of the 9 swept ratios the sign agrees and the slab over-states the magnitude** by
   1.6x-11x (the exception is GBM cisplatin, where the tissue moves 1.6x *more*).
3. **For the 2 taxanes the sign is opposite**, on both comparisons. These are the
   two shortest-`L` drugs in either panel (28.2 / 28.8 um, i.e. 2.4 cells at
   dx = 12) — exactly the drugs whose fields are sharpest — and one of them is
   the repo's only FAIL. **The +8.3 % correction is wrong in sign precisely where
   a verdict depends on it.**
4. **The reason is already inside `boundary_layer_resolution.md`.** Its §6
   established, for the sibling `penetration_index` statistic, that on Xenium
   tissue "something dx-dependent in the reconstruction dominates it" and that
   the Xenium ladder "cannot be read as a convergence statement about the
   solver". The same holds here: a tissue dx ladder is the sum of at least three
   dx-dependent terms — the scoring statistic's discretisation error (what the
   slab isolates), the dx-dependent vessel painting (source integral moves up to
   2.3 % over dx = 8-30, that file's §2.3), and the dx-dependent oxygen and
   cell-state banding (`F_state` 0.5049 / 0.5091 / 0.5152 across
   dx = 18 / 24 / 30 on the breast section; on GBM, `F_state` moves
   0.5305-0.5502 *within* dx = 10-12). The slab measures one term; the ladder
   measures the sum. **That file's §6 and its §8 are in tension with each other,
   and §6 is the one that is right.**

**What survives, and it is the important half.** The slab result is a correct and
valuable measurement of what it actually measures: *the discretisation error of
the scoring statistic, in isolation, with everything else held fixed.* In that
role it is decisive, and it is what §3.1 uses to rank conventions — because
ranking needs a controlled comparison, not a tissue-transferable number. The
finding "p99 is the grid-error bottleneck of the scored ratio" stands
(§1.4 shows it is the *entire* budget at leading order). What does not stand is
"+8.3 % is the bias on the published verdicts".

**And it strengthens the recommendation rather than weakening it.** The tissue's
grid dependence is drug-dependent, non-monotone and, for the sharp-field drugs,
oppositely signed. That is exactly the signature of a **step-function** term
(§3.1) sitting on top of smooth ones. Replacing the order statistic with `tail90`
removes the step-function term from the sum, which is what would make a tissue dx
ladder interpretable at all — and it is a testable prediction (§8).

### 3.6 Selection

| criterion | best | worst | where p99 sits |
|---|---|---|---|
| exact scale invariance (§1.2) | all homogeneous, tied | fixed reference | tied-best |
| grid error at dx = 12, vessel, `f_pen` (§3.1) | tail90 (-0.08 %), moments (<=0.05 %) | p99 (+6.98 %), p95 (+6.67 %) | **worst of the twelve** |
| robustness of that error to the sampling convention (§3.1) | tail90, moments | p90/p95/p99 (step functions) | **worst class** |
| monotone convergence in dx (§3.1) | tail90, moments, p50 | p99.9, max, p95 | non-monotone under population sampling |
| bootstrap variance (§3.3) | mean (0.07-0.25 %) | max (0.33-2.56 %) | 0.14-1.72 %, 2nd worst |
| consistency under halved n (§3.3) | tail90, moments | max (+2.97 %), p99.9 (+0.93 %) | -0.20 %, acceptable |
| spurious response to a compensated D change (§3.2, `recal`) | p50 (0.0009), mean (0.0014) | max (0.0207), p99.9 (0.0151) | 0.0088; tail90 is 31 % tighter |
| sensitivity to a REAL change in field shape (§3.2, `frozen`) | max (0.417), tail99 (0.395) | p50 (0.010), mean (0.128) — nearly blind | 0.385; tail90 keeps 79 % of it |
| fidelity to the stated physical referent (§3.4) | tail90/tail95 (a shell, integrated) | mean/rms/p50 (not upper-tail at all) | above the referent by 1-31 percentiles |

**The moments (mean, rms, exposure-weighted mean) and the median win the pure
accuracy criteria and lose on two independent grounds.** *Definitionally:*
normalising by the mean asserts that the *average* tumour cell sits at the bath
concentration, which describes a well-stirred equilibrated culture, not a
spheroid bathed at its surface — and in the low-dose limit it makes
`f_penetration` identically 1, i.e. it **defines the delivery penalty out of
existence**. *Empirically:* §3.2 shows they are nearly blind to a genuine change
in the perivascular gradient (p50 registers 0.010 and the mean 0.128 against
0.30-0.42 for every upper-tail statistic when `L` really moves). A convention
that is robust because it cannot see the physics is not robust. They are
numerically excellent answers to a different question and are rejected on those
grounds, not on their verdict count.

**`tail90` is the recommendation.** It is the only candidate that is
simultaneously (a) exactly scale-invariant, (b) an upper-tail statistic, so the
bath analogy survives and it keeps 79 % of p99's sensitivity to a real change in
penetration, (c) an integral over a shell rather than a point quantile, so it
inherits the moments' grid behaviour (-0.08 % vs +6.98 % at dx = 12), their
convergence monotonicity, and their insensitivity to the sampling convention, and
(d) roughly half as noisy as p99, with no detectable small-sample bias and 31 %
less leakage of a compensated parameter change. `tail95` is a defensible second
choice — 4x worse on grid error, closer to the shipped value.

**What would change this recommendation.** §3.1's ranking rests on a controlled
1-D problem, and §3.5 has just shown that slab magnitudes do not transfer to
tissue. The ranking is a *relative* claim and is much safer than a magnitude, but
it is not free: if re-scoring the existing tissue dx ladders under `tail90` did
NOT remove their non-monotonicity (§8), the diagnosis would be wrong and this
recommendation should be withdrawn. That test costs no new solves.

| this report §3.1 (controlled slab, exact solution) | the scoring statistic's discretisation error, isolated | **+6.98 % HIGH** vs dx -> 0 | 0.1387 | PASS |
| `grid_convergence_breast.md` §5 (tissue, Richardson, p = 2.95) | the whole pipeline's dx dependence | **-3.4 % LOW** vs the extrapolated limit | 0.1537 | FAIL |
| `grid_convergence_breast.md` §3 (tissue, dx = 12 vs the finest solved grid, dx = 8) | same, without extrapolation | **-2.43 % LOW** | 0.1521 | FAIL |

The band ceiling is **0.1463** and the published value is **0.1484**, a margin of
**+1.5 %**. *Every* estimate of the grid movement is larger than that margin, and
they do not agree on its sign.

Why they differ is §3.5's answer: the slab isolates one term, the tissue ladder
measures a sum of at least three, and docetaxel is one of the two drugs
(`L` = 28.2 um = 2.4 cells at dx = 12) where the terms do not line up. It is not
that one measurement is wrong; they are measurements of different things, and the
one that is closer to the published pipeline — the tissue ladder — is the one
that cannot be decomposed.

**The defensible statement:**

> Breast docetaxel is the only one of the seven scored verdicts whose result is
> a function of the grid at all. Its margin to its band (1.5 %) is smaller than
> every available estimate of the grid movement of the statistic that produces it
> (2.4 %, 3.4 %, 7.0 %), and those estimates disagree in sign. **The verdict is
> UNDETERMINED at dx = 12 um** — not FAIL, and not PASS.

This is deliberately weaker than either correction would license. A proxy-derived
correction that happens to remove the project's only falsification is exactly the
result to hold to the highest bar, and the tissue measurement — the more direct
of the two — points the other way.

**The other eight are safe by a wide margin, under every estimate.** Smallest
margin to the nearest band edge is GBM cisplatin at **+33.8 %** to its lower edge
(and the correction moves it *away* from that edge), then GBM temozolomide at
**-43.2 %** to its upper edge. Every other drug has >= 77 % margin. Their measured
tissue movement over dx = 12 -> 8 is **+0.22 % to +3.79 %** (§3.5) and the slab's
worst-case correction is 9.5 %; neither is within an order of magnitude of those
margins. `results/grid_convergence_gbm.md` and `grid_convergence_breast.md` reach
the same conclusion independently: 5/6 breast and 3/3 GBM verdicts are constant
at every rung of their ladders.

Corrected at finer grids (dx = 8 / 4 / 2 um, slab biases +4.48 / +1.89 / +0.94 %
at 1xIC50) the picture does not change: docetaxel remains the only drug whose
verdict is a function of the grid.

## 7. Recommendation, and what it costs

### 7.1 The recommendation

**Change `BATH_REFERENCE_PCT = 99.0` to a conditional tail mean over the top
10 % of tumour-cell exposure (`tail90`).** Concretely, in
`kill_calibration.decompose`, replace

```python
ref_unit = float(np.percentile(e_unit, reference_pct))
```

with the mean of the cells at or above that percentile, at `reference_pct = 90`.
It is a two-line change, it preserves the function's signature, and it preserves
the exact scale-invariance that §1 shows is the property worth having.

**Justify it in the docstring as follows** (the justification the current
constant lacks): *the bath analogue is a SHELL of well-perfused cells, not a
quantile; averaging over the top decile matches the 17-66 % outer shell of the
spheroids the literature bands come from far better than a top-percentile does,
is a consistent estimator where the max and p99.9 are not, and is an integral over
a region wider than the unresolved 7.5 um perivascular source, which is why its
grid error at the shipped dx = 12 um is -0.08 % against p99's +6.98 %.*

**State a grid band on every scored ratio, and state it MEASURED ON TISSUE, not
from the slab.** §3.5 is explicit that the slab number does not transfer, so the
honest band under the current convention is the one the tissue ladders measure:
**-2.4 % to +3.8 %** over dx = 12 -> 8 across the nine swept ratios
(`grid_convergence_gbm.md`, `grid_convergence_breast.md`), drug-ordered by decay
length and **not one-signed**. The slab's +7.0 % is the right figure for the
*statistic's* contribution to that band and the wrong figure for the band itself.

Under `tail90` the statistic's contribution drops from +6.98 % to **-0.08 %** on
the controlled problem (+0.41 % at dx = 24), i.e. the convention stops being a
term in the tissue band at all. That does not by itself shrink the tissue band —
the reconstruction terms (§3.5 conclusion 4) remain — but it removes the only
*step-function* term from it, which is the precondition for the tissue ladders
having an order of accuracy at all. **That is the concrete, falsifiable payoff of
the change (§8).**

**Do NOT adopt `mean`, `rms`, or the exposure-weighted mean**, despite their
being numerically the best: they are not upper-tail statistics, they answer a
different physical question, and in the low-dose limit the mean makes the
delivery penalty identically 1.

**Do NOT adopt `tail99`, `p99.9`, or `max`**, notwithstanding that the first two
of those would produce more PASSes.

## 8. What this report does NOT establish

* **The slab does not license a correction to any tissue number, and §3.5
  measures the failure rather than asserting it.** It is the right instrument for
  the *mechanism* and for *ranking conventions* — a controlled comparison with an
  exact solution and everything else held fixed — and it is deliberately the same
  instrument the finding came from. It is the wrong instrument for predicting a
  tissue verdict, and this report refuses that use in §6 even where the
  correction would favour the model.
* **The two samplings of the slab bracket the real case rather than reproducing
  it.** Grid-cell weighting has `nx` = 40 samples at dx = 12; the population
  variant has 20,000 at fixed positions; the real sections have 0.09 (GBM) to
  0.34 (breast) tumour cells per tissue grid cell. `tail90` and the moments are
  insensitive to which is used; p90/p95/p99 are not, and their numbers should be
  read as a range, not a point.
* **`tail90` was not tested on the dx ladder on real tissue — and that is the
  one experiment that would settle this.** Two sibling agents own
  `grid_convergence_gbm.md` and `grid_convergence_breast.md`. Their ladders'
  cached exposure fields, re-scored under `tail90`, would cost no new solves and
  would test a sharp prediction: **the GBM panel's non-monotone 6.6-7.4 %
  oscillation below dx = 18 (which its own power-law fit flags as DEGENERATE, with
  no extractable order of accuracy) should collapse, and the two taxanes' opposite
  sign in `grid_convergence_breast.md` should reverse or vanish** — because §3.1
  attributes both to the step-function behaviour of an order statistic on a coarse
  field. If the oscillation survives under `tail90`, the diagnosis in §3.1 and
  §3.5 is wrong and the recommendation should be withdrawn. **Nothing in this
  report tests that, and it is the highest-value next step.**
* **The perivascular referent (§3.4) is a diagnostic, not a proposed convention.**
  A "mean over perfused voxels" normaliser would be structurally exact but would
  make the score depend on the vascular segmentation, which is a separate
  contested input; it is named as an option and not evaluated.
* **Nothing here revisits whether the 3-D:2-D test is worth passing.**
  `kill_calibration.md` §3 already documents that the bands are wide, the
  literature sign is unsettled, and the 2-D reference has a ~19x IQR. A better
  normaliser makes a weak test *numerically clean*; it does not make it strong.
* **No parameter was tuned, and no convention was selected on its verdict count.**
  §3.1-3.5 were computed and the recommendation fixed before §5 was generated;
  the candidate that maximises PASSes (`tail99`, 7/7) is explicitly rejected.
